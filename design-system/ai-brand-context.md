## Purpose

Provide concise, reusable brand context for AI-assisted product and web design,
Figma Make prompting, content drafting, design review, artifact review, visual
context, vendor handoff, and orchestration. Use it to preserve official brand
intent while keeping unsupported digital rules, specifications, and assets out
of generated work.

## Brand Essence

### Direct Brand Guidance

- **Luxury That Lets You In:** Luxury should feel personal, welcoming, and
  confidence-forward—not stiff or performative—and be enabled by technology
  and hospitality. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 4, “Our Strategy Pillars.”
- **A Curated Resort Experience:** Present RWLV as one complete ecosystem of
  hotels, dining, entertainment, wellness, and gaming for a discerning Las
  Vegas visitor. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 4, “Our Strategy Pillars.”
- **Vegas at Your Volume:** Offer the energy of Las Vegas without consuming the
  guest; guests should control their own pace. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 4, “Our Strategy Pillars.”
- RWLV luxury balances energy with ease, excitement with intention, and luxury
  with approachability. It fits how guests choose to move, stay, dine, unwind,
  and experience the city rather than overwhelming them with excess. Source:
  Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 5, “What is luxury?”
- Hotel personalities may vary within the resort: Hilton is familiar,
  dependable, welcoming, and comfortably elevated; Conrad pairs boutique
  character and resort access with purposeful service; Crockfords is
  ultra-private, discreet, and detail-focused. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 11, “Brand Personality Chart.”

### AI Interpretation

- Frame RWLV as an intentional, connected range of experiences rather than a
  collection of louder-is-better attractions.
- Preserve guest agency: show that energy, relaxation, discovery, and privacy
  can coexist and be chosen at the guest’s pace.
- Match a hotel-specific brief to its documented personality without replacing
  the shared RWLV voice.

## Voice And Tone

### Direct Brand Guidance

- The voice is confident without being loud, elevated without stiffness, and
  refined without visible effort. It should communicate ease, intention,
  approachable luxury, and curation. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 7,
  “Tone of Voice.”
- Tonal cues include effortless, understated, thoughtfully designed, refined
  energy, balance, curated, approachable elevation, openness, tailoring,
  connection, and guest-controlled pace. These are personality cues, not a
  required word bank. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 8, “What feels on
  brand.”
- Frame luxury as ease; make the guest feel discerning rather than flashy;
  speak with restraint; prioritize clarity over hype; and use concise,
  intentional language. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 9, “Copy Dos and
  Don’ts.”
- Do not oversell, use cliché Vegas superlatives, equate luxury with
  exclusivity, lean into party culture, sound overly youthful or trendy, or use
  broad “for everyone” messaging. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 9, “Copy Dos
  and Don’ts.”
- Keep the voice consistent while changing emphasis by audience: convenience,
  connectivity, and business-casual ease for convention guests; elevated,
  immersive, social excitement without aggressive casino language for gaming
  guests; curation, discovery, flexibility, and balanced pacing for
  full-experience guests; and experience-led reasons to visit for locals.
  Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 12, “Audience Considerations.”

### AI Interpretation

- Prefer specific, calm, guest-centered statements over inflated descriptors.
- Explain what is curated, connected, convenient, or flexible instead of using
  those qualities as unsupported claims.
- Adapt the message emphasis to the audience, not the underlying personality.
- Avoid treating the cue words on p. 8 as mandatory copy or repeating them
  mechanically across a page.

## Visual Personality

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Photography should create energy and bring the property to life through a
  sense of movement. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 18, “Defining Movement.”
- One proposed visual direction uses double bars, derived from the red tower
  architecture, as a welcoming window into the RWLV experience and a refined,
  ownable identity cue. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 24–25, “A Welcoming
  View.”
- A second proposed direction uses energetic line graphics, derived from the
  building crown, as a visual path across the property and a subtle reference
  to three hotel experiences within one destination. Source: Brand Guidelines
  PDF, pp. 26–27, “Energizing Path.”
- For hotel-offer key art, photography remains the hero. Approved typography,
  purposeful red, pattern, and subtle graphic treatments provide cohesion
  while allowing each offer its own personality. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art System.”
- Building patterns derive from the property façade; logo patterns derive from
  the logo’s curves. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 31–32, “Additional
  Patterns.”

### AI Interpretation

- Let real property or experience imagery carry the visual story; use graphic
  devices as a recognizable RWLV layer, not as the main event.
- Treat “A Welcoming View” and “Energizing Path” as documented alternatives,
  not simultaneous mandatory treatments. Use only the direction authorized by
  the brief or current design system.
- Use building- and logo-derived patterns only from approved assets. Do not
  recreate, approximate, or invent them from the PDF examples.

## Typography Guidance

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Use Tenez as an elegant serif accent that adds a sense of luxury. The guide
  shows Regular, Regular Italic, and Bold. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 14,
  “Accent Font.”
- Retain Avenir Next to maintain brand recognition. Source: Brand Guidelines
  PDF, p. 14, “Accent Font.”
- Use EB Garamond as the web-safe accent fallback in email or other digital
  communications where Tenez will not display. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 15, “Web-Safe Accent Font.”
- The illustrated hierarchy uses an expressive serif H1 with sans-serif
  subtitle and body text. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 16, “Typography.”
- Hotel offer cards should stay within the approved brand font family and use
  consistent rules for headlines, subheads, and supporting copy. Source: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art System.”

### AI Interpretation

- Use Tenez selectively for accent or headline roles and Avenir Next for the
  recognition-bearing sans-serif system; use EB Garamond only when the stated
  fallback condition applies.
- Preserve a clear display, subtitle, and body hierarchy without inventing
  sizes, line heights, tracking, responsive scales, or additional weights.
- Refer to approved font resources; do not embed, distribute, or synthesize
  font files.

## Color Guidance

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Hotel-offer key art should include a purposeful pop of red to connect the
  work to the RWLV palette. The accent may appear through wardrobe, lighting,
  props, food or drink, architecture, graphic bars, or subtle overlays. Source:
  Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art System.”
- The welcoming-window treatment is explicitly inspired by the property’s red
  architectural elements. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 24, “A Welcoming
  View.”
- The guide does not provide reusable color values, a complete palette, color
  ratios, or contrast specifications. Source: complete Brand Guidelines PDF
  review, pp. 1–33.

### AI Interpretation

- Use approved production color tokens. Do not sample colors from the PDF or
  invent hex values.
- **Inference:** Use red selectively as a recognition cue rather than allowing
  it to overpower photography or content. Source basis: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 28, “purposeful pop of red” and photography-first direction.
- Validate accessibility contrast through the production design system; this
  PDF does not define contrast requirements.

## Imagery Guidance

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Carry movement through pool, casino, food, drink, and other property imagery
  so the resort feels alive. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 18, “Defining
  Movement.”
- Relaxing lifestyle photography should feel aspirational through recognition,
  not fantasy: recognizable lifestyle moments that happen at a hotel, not
  idealized people presented as having a better life than the viewer. Source:
  Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 19, “Lifestyle Photography (Relaxing).”
- Energizing nightlife and event photography should feel kinetic, social,
  candid, immersive, warm, and alive. Use movement, flash, close social
  proximity, performance, and service-in-motion to express the shift from
  polished daytime hospitality to electric nightlife rather than an overly
  staged scene. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 20, “Lifestyle Photography
  (Energizing).”
- Food and beverage photography should be warmer and more in situ—people
  enjoying food, food within its restaurant setting, and behind-the-scenes
  makers—rather than dominated by studio setups and black backdrops. Source:
  Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 21, “Food & Beverage Photography.”
- Architectural photography should be warm, lived-in, natural, and not overly
  symmetrical. Include people, subtle movement, and, where appropriate, a
  foreground element that creates a welcoming portal or window. Source: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, p. 22, “Architectural Photography.”
- For hotel-offer key art, photography is the primary visual driver; avoid
  illustration-heavy treatments. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 28, “Hotel
  Offer Key Art System.”

### AI Interpretation

- Choose imagery whose energy matches the moment: ease and recognition for
  relaxation; candid proximity and movement for nightlife; warmth, makers, and
  context for dining; people and lived-in depth for architecture.
- Keep the place, offering, or human moment legible even when using blur,
  movement, flash, overlays, or foreground framing.
- Do not treat PDF examples as a distributable image library. Use only approved,
  rights-cleared assets, and label generated or placeholder imagery as such.

## Layout And Composition Guidance

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Graphic treatments such as blending, lines, highlights, textured
  backgrounds, subtle frames, and patterns should enhance photography rather
  than overpower it. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art
  System.”
- Patterns should be a subtle layer—such as an overlay, gradient texture,
  background detail, or transition—and must not compete with the photograph or
  offer message. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art
  System.”
- The design examples use framing and path devices to create a window into the
  experience or guide the eye across the property story. Source: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, pp. 24–27, “A Welcoming View” and “Energizing Path.”
- The guide does not define a web grid, spacing scale, responsive behavior,
  component dimensions, or page-level composition system. Source: complete
  Brand Guidelines PDF review, pp. 1–33.

### AI Interpretation

- Establish a clear image-and-message relationship before adding a graphic
  treatment.
- **Inference:** Use a framing device to invite focus or a line device to imply
  movement and connection; do not add both by default. Source basis: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, pp. 24–27.
- Preserve legibility and hierarchy when adapting campaign-style examples to
  product or web surfaces. Do not treat static examples as responsive rules.

## Hospitality Experience Principles

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Hospitality should make luxury feel personal, welcoming, and
  confidence-forward. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 4, “Luxury That Lets You
  In.”
- The resort experience should feel curated and connected across hotels,
  dining, entertainment, wellness, and gaming. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF,
  p. 4, “A Curated Resort Experience.”
- Guests should be able to engage with Las Vegas energy without being consumed
  by it and retain control over pace. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 4–5,
  “Vegas at Your Volume” and “What is luxury?”
- Messaging should make the guest feel discerning rather than flashy. Source:
  Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 9, “Copy Dos and Don’ts.”

### AI Interpretation

- Help guests understand the range and relationship of experiences while
  preserving freedom to choose their own level of energy and engagement.
- Express premium hospitality through ease, clarity, and intention rather than
  exclusion, excess, or status performance.
- Use technology as an enabler of the welcome and experience, not as the lead
  brand story unless the brief requires it.

## Web And Landing Page Implications

### Direct Brand Guidance

- The PDF defines a web-safe serif fallback for environments where Tenez does
  not display and provides offer-card/key-art guidance, but it does not define
  a comprehensive website, landing-page, component, responsive, accessibility,
  or interaction system. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 15 and 28.
- In offer work, approved typography should create cohesion, photography should
  stay primary, red should be purposeful, patterns should remain subtle, and
  illustration-heavy treatments should be avoided. Source: Brand Guidelines
  PDF, p. 28, “Hotel Offer Key Art System.”

### AI Interpretation

- **Inference:** Organize a page around one clear experience promise and show
  how relevant hotels, dining, entertainment, wellness, or gaming choices form
  a curated whole. Source basis: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 4–5.
- **Inference:** Balance high-energy modules with quieter, ease-oriented
  moments so the page supports “Vegas at Your Volume.” Source basis: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, pp. 4–5 and 18–22.
- Prefer approved photography, typography, red accents, and authorized graphic
  devices over invented luxury motifs.
- Use the production design system for layout, responsive behavior,
  accessibility, components, and exact tokens. Do not derive those rules from
  campaign examples in the PDF.

## CTA And Interaction Guidance

### Direct Brand Guidance

- CTA-adjacent copy should follow the same voice rules: concise, intentional,
  clear rather than hyped, and restrained rather than oversold. Source: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, pp. 7–10, “Copy & Voice.”
- “Book Now” appears as an action in visual-treatment examples, but the guide
  does not define a CTA taxonomy, hierarchy, placement system, state model, or
  interaction behavior. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 24–26, design-recipe
  examples.

### AI Interpretation

- Use plain, specific action language and avoid cliché superlatives, party-led
  urgency, exclusion, or exaggerated promises.
- **Inference:** Give enough context for the guest to choose confidently before
  asking for commitment, reflecting luxury as ease and guest control. Source
  basis: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 4–5 and 9.
- Follow the production design system for button hierarchy, placement, states,
  motion, and accessibility; the PDF does not authorize exact interaction
  rules.

## Anti-Patterns

### Direct Brand Guidance

- Loud, stiff, effortful, or exclusion-led expressions of luxury. Source: Brand
  Guidelines PDF, pp. 4–7.
- Overselling; cliché Vegas superlatives; aggressive casino language; party
  culture as the defining story; overly youthful or trendy language; and vague
  “for everyone” messaging. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 9 and 12.
- Aspirational lifestyle imagery based on fantasy, status distance, or visibly
  staged perfection. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 19–20.
- Cold studio food photography dominated by black backdrops, or architectural
  photography that is overly symmetrical and lacks people or subtle movement.
  Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, pp. 21–22.
- Offer systems with inconsistent fonts, graphic treatments that overpower the
  image, illustration-heavy identities, or patterns that compete with the
  photograph or message. Source: Brand Guidelines PDF, p. 28.

### AI Interpretation

- Do not turn every brand cue—serif type, red, movement, lines, bars, and
  patterns—on at once. That would undermine the documented restraint,
  photographic priority, and purposeful use of graphic layers.
- Do not use red without a clear brand or compositional purpose.
- Do not fabricate RWLV patterns, font substitutes, logo-derived graphics,
  photography, color values, or hotel personality rules.
- Do not apply high-energy nightlife direction to every guest, page, or moment;
  preserve the documented balance of energy and ease.

## Figma Make Brand Context Block

```text
RESORTS WORLD LAS VEGAS BRAND CONTEXT

Create an RWLV experience that feels personal, welcoming, confidence-forward,
curated, and elevated without stiffness. Balance Las Vegas energy with ease and
let guests control the pace. Use concise, intentional copy: clarity over hype,
invitation over overselling, and discerning over flashy. Avoid cliché Vegas
superlatives, aggressive casino language, exclusion-led luxury, party-culture
shorthand, trendy youth language, and vague “for everyone” claims.

Use approved RWLV typography: Tenez as the serif accent, Avenir Next for brand
recognition, and EB Garamond only as the stated digital fallback when Tenez
cannot display. Do not invent font files, type values, or substitutes.

Keep approved photography primary. Show recognizable, lived-in experiences with
movement: relaxed lifestyle through relatable hotel moments; nightlife through
candid warmth, social proximity, and kinetic energy; food in its restaurant or
making context; architecture with people, natural warmth, subtle motion, and
welcoming depth. Avoid fantasy-luxury staging, cold studio food, empty symmetry,
and illustration-heavy offer art.

Use approved production colors and assets. Apply red purposefully as a selective
brand cue; do not sample or invent color values. Use only the authorized
welcoming-window, energetic-line, building-pattern, or logo-pattern assets.
Graphic treatments and patterns must support—not overpower—the photograph and
message.

[Inference] For web compositions, present one clear experience promise, connect
the relevant resort choices as a curated whole, and balance energetic and quiet
moments. Use the production design system for grids, spacing, components,
responsive behavior, contrast, interaction states, and accessibility. Do not
invent offers, claims, assets, or specifications absent from the brief.
```
