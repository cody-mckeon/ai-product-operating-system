# Figma Make Context

## Purpose

This document provides reusable RWLV context for Figma Make prompts. It helps future agents generate brand-consistent explorations while preserving existing website context.

This document does not create designs, wireframes, implementation specifications, or engineering requirements.

## What RWLV Should Feel Like

RWLV should feel:

- Welcoming and premium.
- Cinematic but usable.
- Curated rather than crowded.
- Confident without shouting.
- Editorial without becoming magazine-only.
- Commercially clear without feeling pressured.
- Energetic at the guest's chosen pace.
- Operationally trustworthy.

## What RWLV Should Not Feel Like

RWLV should not feel:

- Like a generic SaaS template.
- Like a full-page ad when the guest needs planning support.
- Like a casino overload of neon, glow, motion, and competing offers.
- Like a luxury brand that is cold, stiff, or inaccessible.
- Like a disconnected landing page inserted into an existing website.
- Like every module is competing to be the hero.
- Like proof, social content, partner marks, or offers are decorative filler.

## Visual Style

Use composed restraint, cinematic hospitality, atmospheric depth, editorial hierarchy, and warm premium clarity.

Figma Make should:

- Preserve recognizable RWLV page context.
- Use imagery and spacing to create premium presence.
- Keep commercial actions clear and subordinate to guest understanding when appropriate.
- Avoid decorative effects that overwhelm content.
- Treat modules as part of a larger guest journey, not isolated campaign blocks.

## Hierarchy Expectations

Hierarchy should make relationships clear.

Use:

- One dominant message per section.
- Clear heading levels.
- Short contextual labels when they clarify category, venue, package, offer, or proof role.
- Supporting copy that explains why the section matters.
- CTAs placed after enough context has been established.

Avoid:

- Multiple equal-weight modules competing for attention.
- CTA-first layouts that skip orientation.
- Display-scale type inside compact panels.
- Repeating the same content hierarchy across every card or section.

## Imagery Direction

Use imagery that shows the property, experience, venue, food, hotel, or guest moment clearly.

Confirmed brand guideline direction supports movement and energy in photography.

Figma Make should:

- Prefer real-feeling hospitality and experience imagery over abstract atmosphere.
- Use entertainment imagery that conveys venue energy without overwhelming readability.
- Use hotel and package imagery to support trust, comfort, and arrival.
- Crop with intention so the subject remains legible.

Do not rely on:

- Generic stock-like luxury imagery.
- Dark, blurred, or overly cropped visuals when guests need to inspect the experience.
- Purely decorative background imagery as a substitute for content.

## Spacing Principles

Spacing should create calm momentum.

Use spacing to:

- Separate journey stages.
- Group related content.
- Let premium modules breathe.
- Support scanning in dense areas.
- Preserve continuity between inserted modules and surrounding sections.

Do not use spacing to:

- Make every section feel like a standalone landing page.
- Inflate minor content into oversized hero moments.
- Hide weak content behind excessive emptiness.

## Component Expectations

Figma Make should prefer existing or reusable website patterns when the task is an enhancement.

Reusable pattern families include:

- Experience Hero.
- Editorial Feature.
- Benefit Validation Module.
- Venue Orientation Feature.
- Event Bridge.
- Event Card.
- Venue Card.
- Offer Card.
- Package Card.
- CTA Block.
- Booking Bar or booking handoff.
- Page Anchor Navigation.
- FAQ or support continuity module.
- Social Proof Layer.

Adapt components to the content role. Do not invent new component categories when an existing pattern can carry the job.

## Content Density Guidance

RWLV can support both atmosphere and utility.

Use:

- Lower density for arrival, orientation, and emotional framing.
- Moderate density for venue, package, offer, and benefit discovery.
- Higher but organized density for event listings, comparison, terms, FAQs, and operational details.

Avoid:

- Card walls with no hierarchy.
- Long narrative blocks where guests need comparison.
- Overly sparse modules that force extra scrolling without adding clarity.

## Accessibility Expectations

Figma Make prompts should preserve:

- Semantic heading order.
- Readable copy.
- Clear link and button intent.
- Keyboard-friendly structures for anchors, accordions, and filters.
- Contrast-aware text placement over imagery.
- Reduced-motion-compatible interaction posture.

## Inserted Module Guidance

When exploring enhancements inside existing pages:

- Preserve the global header, footer, existing discovery paths, and existing conversion paths unless the brief explicitly says otherwise.
- Insert the new module at the recommended journey point.
- Match surrounding page rhythm and component language.
- Avoid standalone landing-page drift.
- Keep the enhancement focused on its job: orientation, proof, discovery, decision support, or continuity.
- Do not redesign unrelated page sections.

## Figma Make Prompt Constraints

Future prompts should include:

- The page context being preserved.
- The inserted module's role in the guest journey.
- Confirmed content relationships.
- Which existing patterns should be reused or adapted.
- Which claims, assets, or governance details are unknown.
- A reminder not to invent events, offers, partner claims, package inclusions, or social proof.

Future prompts should avoid:

- Full-page redesign language unless explicitly requested.
- Exact token values unless confirmed by source materials.
- Engineering implementation instructions.
- Final design claims.
- Wireframe-specific direction when the task is conceptual exploration.
