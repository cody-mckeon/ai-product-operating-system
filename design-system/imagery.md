# Imagery

## Purpose

This document provides reusable RWLV imagery guidance for AI-assisted design workflows.

It does not specify final image selections, licensing decisions, asset production requirements, or implementation details.

## Evidence Sources

Confirmed by brand guidelines:

- `design-system/brand-guidelines/rwlv-brand-guidelines-2026.pdf`
- The guidelines include photography categories for lifestyle, food and beverage, and architecture.
- The guidelines indicate imagery should bring the property to life and may incorporate movement and energy.

Confirmed by brand doctrine:

- `design-system/brand.md`
- `design-system/components.md`

Observed in pilot outputs:

- Conrad pilot emphasized package value, club/dining benefits, and proof adjacency.
- Entertainment pilot emphasized venue orientation, event discovery, and restrained social context.

Unknown:

- Approved production image library, current image rights, exact crops, and channel-specific art direction rules.

## Imagery Personality

RWLV imagery should feel cinematic, hospitable, energetic, polished, and specific to the resort experience.

Use imagery to:

- Show the actual place, experience, venue, room, dish, or moment.
- Create a sense of arrival.
- Support discovery and decision-making.
- Communicate energy without overwhelming the guest.

## Photography Styles

Reusable styles:

- Hospitality lifestyle: guests at ease, welcomed, and in control of the pace.
- Energized lifestyle: entertainment, nightlife, and resort energy with clear subject focus.
- Food and beverage: appetite, craft, materiality, and setting.
- Architecture: scale, texture, arrival, and spatial identity.
- Experience detail: evidence of what the guest receives or can do.

## Hospitality Imagery

Use:

- Welcoming arrival moments.
- Service cues that feel natural rather than staged.
- Spaces that show comfort, material quality, and calm momentum.
- Guest moments that feel personal and inclusive.

Avoid:

- Stiff luxury posing.
- Empty spaces that feel cold.
- Generic service imagery that could belong to any property.

## Entertainment Imagery

Use:

- Venue energy with legible performers, crowd, stage, or environment.
- Movement that supports the entertainment story.
- Clear cues for whether the experience is concert, nightlife, theater, or live programming.
- Imagery that helps guests understand venue mood and scale.

Avoid:

- Overly dark concert imagery where the venue or subject disappears.
- Abstract lights as a substitute for event or venue context.
- Social-looking imagery without governance or source confidence.

## Food Imagery

Use:

- Food that feels crafted, textural, and appetizing.
- Restaurant context when it helps guests understand the experience.
- Human cues when they add hospitality and scale.

Avoid:

- Generic plated-food shots without environment or personality.
- Crops that obscure the dish or make it difficult to understand.
- Overly stylized images that feel disconnected from the actual dining experience.

## Hotel Imagery

Use:

- Rooms, suites, and hotel spaces that show comfort, material quality, light, and stay value.
- Arrival and relaxation cues.
- Details that validate package or premium claims.

Avoid:

- Room imagery that is too cropped to understand the space.
- Empty luxury shots that feel sterile.
- Imagery that promises an inclusion or access level not confirmed in content.

## Venue Imagery

Use:

- Establishing views that clarify the venue.
- Detail shots that communicate mood, scale, and guest experience.
- Images that distinguish one venue from another.

Avoid:

- Treating every venue as the same card image format when their roles differ.
- Crops that remove signage, stage, architecture, or other identity cues.
- Imagery that implies an inaccurate relationship between venues or programs.

## Experience Imagery

Use:

- Images that help guests imagine participating, attending, staying, reserving, or booking.
- Proof-oriented images when benefits or inclusions need validation.
- Sequential imagery when planning or package value depends on multiple moments.

Avoid:

- Pure mood imagery where guests need practical clarity.
- Decorative photography that does not advance understanding.

## Cropping And Composition

Cropping should preserve subject clarity.

Guidance:

- Keep the main subject readable across desktop and mobile contexts.
- Preserve environmental cues when place identity matters.
- Use close crops for materiality and detail only when context is available nearby.
- Avoid placing text over visually busy image areas.

## Movement And Energy

Confirmed brand guideline direction supports movement and energy in imagery.

Use movement to:

- Bring the property to life.
- Suggest entertainment, hospitality, and guest momentum.
- Add vitality without creating visual chaos.

Avoid:

- Motion blur that obscures the subject.
- Energy cues that feel generic or disconnected from RWLV.

## DO

- Use specific RWLV-relevant imagery.
- Show real experiences, spaces, products, venues, and guest moments.
- Match image energy to the guest journey stage.
- Use imagery as evidence when claims need validation.
- Preserve enough clarity for guests to understand what is being offered.
- Confirm rights, approvals, and governance before using partner, press, or social imagery.

## DO NOT

- Use generic stock-like luxury imagery.
- Use dark, blurred, or overly cropped imagery when guests need to inspect the experience.
- Use abstract casino visuals as a default Las Vegas shorthand.
- Invent imagery for events, packages, inclusions, or venues that are not confirmed.
- Let image atmosphere overpower accessibility or readability.

## Examples

Reusable example directions:

- Entertainment: a clear view of a stage, performer, or venue environment that communicates energy and place.
- Package validation: a benefit image that shows the experience or access being described.
- Dining: a dish with enough setting context to feel like an RWLV restaurant moment.
- Hotel: a room or suite image that supports comfort, arrival, and stay value.
- Venue orientation: an establishing image that helps guests recognize the venue and understand its role.

These are directional examples, not asset prescriptions.

## Asset Availability Notes

Future agents should document when an image is confirmed, inferred, placeholder, generated, or unavailable.

Do not treat generated or placeholder imagery as approved production photography.
