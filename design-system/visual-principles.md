# Visual Principles

## Purpose

This document translates RWLV brand doctrine and pilot learnings into reusable visual guidance for AI-assisted product and design workflows.

It does not define final layouts, visual specs, token values, or implementation requirements.

## Evidence Sources

Confirmed by brand doctrine:

- `design-system/brand.md`
- `design-system/tokens.md`
- `design-system/components.md`

Confirmed by brand guidelines:

## Visual Personality

RWLV should look composed, premium, warm, cinematic, and operationally clear.

Confirmed guidance:

- Luxury should feel personal and welcoming.
- The resort should feel curated and experience-rich.
- Guests should feel energized without being overwhelmed.

AI guidance:

- Let the page feel polished through hierarchy, pacing, imagery, and precision.
- Use restraint as a luxury signal.
- Make useful information feel designed, not merely displayed.

## Composition Principles

Use composition to create calm momentum.

Principles:

- Give each section a clear role.
- Establish one primary focal point at a time.
- Preserve existing page context when inserting new modules.
- Use asymmetry or editorial framing when it clarifies story and emphasis.
- Keep dense information organized into readable groups.
- Avoid layouts where every item has equal visual weight.

## Hierarchy Principles

Hierarchy should clarify the guest journey.

Principles:

- Arrival content should orient before selling.
- Discovery content should reveal options without flattening them.
- Evaluation content should compare, validate, or explain.
- Decision content should make action clear and respectful.
- Support content should answer practical questions without interrupting primary merchandising.

Avoid:

- Multiple display-scale headings in one viewport.
- Repeating identical card hierarchy for content with different importance.
- Hiding critical information under atmospheric styling.

## Spacing And Pacing

Spacing should communicate confidence, not emptiness.

Principles:

- Use generous space for arrival and orientation.
- Use tighter, organized space for event listings, FAQs, terms, and comparison.
- Give proof and decision-support content enough proximity to the claim it supports.
- Preserve section rhythm when adding inserted modules.
- Let content density rise gradually as guests move from inspiration to action.

## Color Usage

Use color as a hierarchy and atmosphere tool.

Confirmed and inferred guidance:

- RWLV uses a premium hospitality palette supported by warm neutrals, editorial depth, and action emphasis.
- Red is a recognizable brand and action signal in reviewed materials, but exact color values are not confirmed here.
- Accent color should clarify action or identity, not dominate every surface.

Avoid:

- One-note palettes that make the page feel flat.
- Heavy neon effects as a default Las Vegas cue.
- Overuse of dark luxury styling that reduces warmth or readability.
- Decorative gradients and glows that compete with content.

## Motion And Interaction Posture

Motion should be purposeful, calm, and optional.

Principles:

- Use motion to clarify transitions, reveal structure, or support continuity.
- Avoid motion that feels like spectacle detached from guest need.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep interaction states clear without relying only on animation.

## Premium Restraint

Premium restraint means giving content the confidence to breathe while remaining useful.

Use restraint to:

- Reduce visual noise.
- Make commercial actions feel trustworthy.
- Let photography and typography carry the experience.
- Avoid over-explaining in the interface.

Do not use restraint to:

- Remove necessary operational clarity.
- Hide terms, eligibility, venue relationships, or action intent.
- Make pages feel empty or under-merchandised.

## Accessibility Considerations

Visual guidance should preserve:

- Clear reading order.
- Sufficient contrast.
- Legible text over imagery.
- Distinguishable interactive states.
- Scannable section labels and headings.
- Accessible FAQ, anchor, and card structures.

## Visual Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Generic luxury templates.
- Generic SaaS card grids.
- Full-page redesign drift when the task is an enhancement.
- Partner or social content used as decoration.
- Equal-weight merchandising of content with different business or guest priority.
- Imagery that is too dark, blurred, cropped, or atmospheric to clarify the experience.
