# Executive Summary

## Primary Concept

**CON-01 — Meet Savor Society**

An editorial orientation experience is the clearest, most durable answer to the business question. It establishes Savor Society as a cross-venue culinary and cocktail collective, makes the program discoverable from Dining, and preserves Dining's primary role as a venue-discovery and reservation experience.

## Supporting Concepts

- **CON-02 — Savor Through the Venues** provides the venue-to-program relationship needed to make Savor Society relevant rather than merely promotional.
- **CON-06 — Connected Culinary Context** provides the reciprocal journey logic needed to preserve context between Dining, Savor Society, participating venues, and distinct reservation paths.

## Excluded Concepts

- **CON-03 — A Savor Moment Now** is rejected from the selected direction because it depends on a single current program, carries the highest freshness burden, and has the greatest risk of becoming a promotional banner or campaign feature.

CON-04 and CON-05 remain Tier 2 supporting opportunities but do not advance as standalone Design Review directions.

## Selection Rationale

The selected direction combines CON-01, CON-02, and CON-06 into one coherent experience:

1. Orient guests to what Savor Society is.
2. Clarify how the collective relates to participating Dining venues.
3. Preserve context and action intent as guests move between Dining and Savor Society.

This combination is stronger than any concept alone. CON-01 supplies stable collective-level understanding but would be too abstract without venue evidence. CON-02 supplies relevance through venues but would be fragmented without umbrella orientation. CON-06 supplies journey continuity but needs the other two concepts to define what context is being carried. Together they strengthen awareness without repositioning Dining around programming, duplicating the Savor Society page, or depending on a single event.

# Business Problem Prioritization

## Priority 1 — Culinary-programming awareness without disrupting Dining

Savor Society is absent from the Dining body, but Dining already performs a clear venue-discovery and reservation function. The primary problem is making Savor Society discoverable while keeping restaurant discovery dominant.

Evidence strength: High.

## Priority 2 — Collective, program, and venue relationship clarity

Savor Society spans multiple restaurants, lounges, chefs, mixologists, dated events, and recurring experiences. Guests need to understand that it is a collective operating through venues, not a venue or dining category.

Evidence strength: High.

## Priority 3 — Restaurant, event, detail, and signup intent continuity

Dining and Savor Society expose different commitments: venue detail, restaurant reservation, event reservation, program exploration, list signup, and hotel booking. These paths need to remain distinct as programming gains visibility.

Evidence strength: Medium.

## Priority 4 — Cross-page continuity

The current relationship is one-way: Savor Society names host venues, while Dining does not expose Savor Society. Guests can lose context when moving between program-led and venue-led surfaces.

Evidence strength: High.

## Priority 5 — Governance and freshness resilience

Savor Society content changes more frequently than the Dining venue directory. Any selected direction must remain useful as programming changes and avoid dependence on constantly refreshed event inventory where possible.

Evidence strength: High.

# Primary Concept Selection

- Savor Society has no observed body presence on Dining.
- Dining remains a categorized venue inventory with filters and reservations.
- Savor Society is a collective spanning chef collaborations, mixology programming, and recurring experiences.
- The design system supports Editorial Feature and Venue Orientation roles for contextual explanation.
- The recommendation identifies Savor Society as Dining's programming layer rather than a venue or category.

The concept increases culinary-programming awareness among dining-interested guests without making Dining program-led. It preserves venue and reservation discovery while establishing a qualified path to the canonical Savor Society experience.

## Risks

- Incorrect prominence could disrupt Dining hierarchy.
- Generic copy or imagery could make the experience feel promotional.
- Cross-page and venue-level continuity require support from CON-02 and CON-06.

# Unified Design Direction

### Experience Direction

Create a restrained Dining-to-Savor orientation experience that introduces Savor Society as Resorts World Las Vegas's cross-venue culinary and cocktail collective, demonstrates that relationship through participating venues, and preserves context between Dining and the canonical Savor Society experience.

Dining remains the place to discover and reserve restaurants. Savor Society becomes a clearly related programming layer for guests who want chef collaborations, mixology takeovers, recurring rituals, and other culinary experiences.

### User Problem Solved

Guests can understand that culinary programming exists, what Savor Society is, how it relates to venues they recognize, and which path continues to venue details, restaurant reservations, program details, event reservations, or future-interest signup.

### Core Pattern

Editorial Orientation Feature, based on the documented Editorial Feature and Venue Orientation Feature roles.

### Supporting Patterns

- Venue-to-program relationship context using Venue Card and Venue Orientation roles.
- Program-to-venue continuity using Event Bridge and contextual-link roles.
- Focused program-discovery action using CTA Block principles.
- Cross-page orientation using existing wayfinding and reversible-exploration behaviors where production capability is confirmed.

### Content Role

Content defines Savor Society in precise guest language, identifies it as a collective rather than a venue or category, explains the kinds of programming it encompasses, and uses governed participating-venue evidence to make the relationship concrete. The Dining surface provides orientation and relevance; the Savor Society surface remains the source for deeper program discovery.

### Trust Role

Trust comes from accurate venue relationships, current and governed claims, specific CTA intent, appropriate imagery rights, and clear separation between stable venue content and changing program information. Press context can validate positioning but does not become guest-facing proof by default.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests discover Savor Society without losing the Dining journey, understand its cross-venue role, and move to the correct venue, program, reservation, or signup context with confidence.

### Expected Business Outcome

Savor Society gains qualified awareness and cross-page engagement while restaurant discovery and reservation continuity remain protected. The direction can scale as programming changes because it is anchored in collective orientation and governed venue relationships rather than one event.

# Design Exploration Brief

Design Review should explore one unified direction based on CON-01 with support from CON-02 and CON-06.

The exploration should determine how a restrained editorial orientation can make Savor Society visible within the existing Dining journey after Dining's venue-led purpose is clear. It should show enough participating-venue relationship evidence to make the collective concrete, while leaving filters, venue discovery, venue details, and restaurant reservations dominant.

Design Review should test whether the direction:

- Orients guests to Savor Society before presenting a program action.
- Makes Savor Society legible as a cross-venue collective rather than a restaurant, venue, or dining category.
- Preserves the existing Dining hierarchy and avoids a second hero, banner behavior, or equal-weight event grid.
- Clarifies participating-venue relationships without overloading venue discovery.
- Distinguishes program discovery, venue details, restaurant reservations, event reservations, list signup, and hotel booking.
- Supports reciprocal context between Dining and Savor Society without duplicating the Savor Society page or an event calendar.
- Uses established Editorial Feature, Venue Orientation, Venue Card, Event Bridge, and CTA Block roles where production reuse is feasible.
- Remains credible when no marquee event is available and scales as programming changes.
- Uses approved food, beverage, talent, venue, or experience imagery only when it clarifies the relationship.
- Preserves semantic hierarchy, mobile scanning, accessible actions, and RWLV's warm, editorial, restrained hospitality posture.

Design Review must not invent event inventory, dates, availability, list benefits, venue participation, component capabilities, or operational states. A production component inventory, rendered responsive review, canonical program data, ownership model, asset rights, and reservation-handoff review remain required evidence inputs.
