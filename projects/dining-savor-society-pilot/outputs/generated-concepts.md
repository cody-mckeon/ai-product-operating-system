# Generated Concepts

## Portfolio Guardrails

All concepts originate from REC-01, REC-02, and REC-03. Across the portfolio:

- Dining remains a venue-led restaurant-discovery experience.
- Savor Society remains a cross-venue culinary and cocktail collective.
- Restaurant filters, venue discovery, venue details, and restaurant reservations remain primary Dining functions.
- Savor Society is not treated as a restaurant, venue, dining category, loyalty proposition, package, standalone landing page, or replacement hero.
- Program discovery remains distinct from restaurant reservation, event reservation, list signup, and generic “Learn More” intent.
- Concepts do not duplicate the full Savor Society page or create a second event calendar.
- Dates, programs, venues, availability, benefits, and operating capabilities remain limited to governed source content.

## Concept 1

### Concept ID

CON-01

### Concept Name

Meet Savor Society

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Editorial Orientation Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests browsing Dining have no current body-level explanation that a year-round culinary collective exists or how it differs from the restaurant directory.

### Component Pattern

Editorial Orientation Feature

### Content Strategy

A concise, guest-centered definition of Savor Society explains that the collective brings chef collaborations, mixology takeovers, and recurring experiences to multiple Resorts World venues. Governed food, beverage, talent, or hospitality imagery establishes its culinary relevance. A program-discovery action remains distinct from restaurant reservations and list signup.

### Placement Strategy

This experience belongs at a secondary orientation point within Dining, after guests understand that the page's primary purpose is restaurant discovery but before the journey becomes entirely venue-list driven. It does not replace the page's arrival content, filters, or category hierarchy.

### Why It Is Different

This concept solves the problem through a single umbrella-level explanation. It does not depend on a specific current event, recurring schedule, participating-venue network, or guided choice among actions.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Establish awareness and correct classification of Savor Society without repositioning Dining around programming.

### Experience Description

Guests encounter a clear introduction to Savor Society as an additional layer of dining discovery. The experience first answers what the collective is and why it belongs in a dining context. It then offers a program-oriented continuation to the canonical Savor Society surface. Restaurant browsing remains available without requiring guests to engage with the collective.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can recognize Savor Society, understand its relationship to Dining, and decide whether culinary programming is relevant to them without losing the restaurant-discovery path.

### Expected Business Outcome

Savor Society gains qualified awareness among Dining visitors while Dining retains its established venue and reservation purpose.

### Benefits

- Gives unfamiliar guests a clear mental model before asking for action.
- Establishes the collective as cross-venue rather than category- or venue-specific.
- Uses a documented Editorial Feature and Venue Orientation role.
- Can remain useful even when individual event inventory changes.
- Keeps conversion pressure subordinate to understanding.

### Risks

- Broad editorial language could become decorative or too vague to change discoverability.
- Excessive prominence could compete with restaurant discovery.
- Insufficient program specificity could make Savor Society feel like a campaign label rather than an active collective.
- Imagery could imply unconfirmed venues, talent, or programming if not governed.

### Constraints

- Dining remains the dominant page identity.
- The experience cannot function as a second hero or a banner-only promotion.
- Content must identify Savor Society as a collective, not a restaurant, venue, or category.
- No unconfirmed dates, events, benefits, or membership claims may be introduced.
- The canonical Savor Society page remains the deeper program destination.

## Concept 2

### Concept ID

CON-02

### Concept Name

Savor Through the Venues

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer
- REC-02 — Make venue and program relationships reciprocal
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Contextual Venue Relationship Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests evaluating a restaurant cannot currently tell whether that venue hosts relevant Savor Society programming, while program visitors may not understand the host venue's wider dining identity.

### Component Pattern

Venue-to-Program Relationship Layer

### Content Strategy

Participating venues receive governed relationship content that identifies the relevant Savor Society program type, current or recurring timing when confirmed, and the distinction between venue details and program details. Reciprocal Savor Society content identifies the host venue with the same naming and relationship language.

### Placement Strategy

This experience belongs within the venue-evaluation journey for confirmed participating venues and within the corresponding program context on Savor Society. It follows guest interest in a specific venue rather than introducing programming as a separate dining category.

### Why It Is Different

This concept begins with a restaurant or lounge and reveals its program relationship contextually. Unlike an umbrella introduction or current-program feature, it distributes discovery through participating venues and makes the relationship reciprocal.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Use existing venue interest to make Savor Society programming relevant while preserving venue-led Dining discovery.

### Experience Description

As guests evaluate a participating venue, they can recognize that the venue hosts a Savor Society collaboration, takeover, or recurring experience. The experience distinguishes the venue's stable identity and standard restaurant-reservation path from the program's timing, detail, and event action. On Savor Society, the host venue remains equally clear and connects back to its broader dining context.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests understand where a program occurs, how it relates to a venue they are considering, and whether they want venue information, a restaurant reservation, or program details.

### Expected Business Outcome

Venue demand and programming awareness reinforce one another, supporting cross-discovery without creating a parallel venue directory.

### Benefits

- Grounds Savor Society in familiar, concrete dining venues.
- Makes the current one-way venue relationship reciprocal.
- Preserves restaurant reservation access for guests with venue-first intent.
- Supports relevant discovery without exposing every Dining visitor to equal program weight.
- Scales across fine dining, bars, Famous Foods, and hotel-hosted experiences when relationships are confirmed.

### Risks

- Program relationships could become stale or imply continuous participation.
- Additional venue context could increase card or detail density.
- Guests could confuse an event reservation with a normal restaurant reservation.
- Cross-surface naming could drift if venue and program content have different owners.

### Constraints

- Only confirmed participating venues may carry a Savor Society relationship.
- The venue remains the primary identity within Dining.
- Program and restaurant actions must remain semantically distinct.
- The concept cannot require duplicate program records or invented venue capabilities.
- Expired, postponed, sold-out, and inactive relationships require governed treatment.

## Concept 3

### Concept ID

CON-03

### Concept Name

A Savor Moment Now

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer
- REC-02 — Make venue and program relationships reciprocal
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Curated Programming Feature Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests may understand that culinary programming exists but still lack a concrete, current example that connects Savor Society, a host venue, decision-support information, and the appropriate next action.

### Component Pattern

Editorial Event Bridge

### Content Strategy

One governed Savor Society moment provides a complete story: program or talent identity, host venue, confirmed date or cadence, concise value framing, relevant operational details, and a clearly differentiated program action. Supporting imagery shows the actual food, beverage, talent, venue, or guest experience when approved.

### Placement Strategy

This experience belongs at a secondary discovery point on Dining where a current culinary story can deepen interest without interrupting filters or replacing the venue inventory. The canonical Savor Society surface carries broader programming discovery.

### Why It Is Different

This concept uses one current, complete program story rather than explaining the full collective, distributing relationships across many venues, or presenting recurring-program cadence. It creates depth through a single governed example rather than breadth.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Turn abstract awareness into qualified interest through a tangible example of Savor Society in action.

### Experience Description

Guests move from understanding that Dining offers many venues to seeing one timely expression of the resort's culinary programming. The feature explains why the moment is part of Savor Society, identifies its host venue, and distinguishes program exploration or event reservation from the venue's standard reservation path. Guests can continue restaurant discovery without engaging.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can understand what a Savor Society experience looks like in practice and determine whether a current program is relevant to their visit or interests.

### Expected Business Outcome

Current programming receives focused, qualified exposure while the full event inventory remains governed on the canonical Savor Society page.

### Benefits

- Makes the collective tangible through a complete example.
- Supports strong program orientation before conversion.
- Avoids duplicating an event calendar.
- Creates clear separation between current programming and stable venue inventory.
- Aligns with documented Editorial Feature, Event Bridge, and CTA Block roles.

### Risks

- A single feature may overrepresent one venue, talent, or program type.
- Frequent updates may create editorial and operational burden.
- Stale dates, availability, or reservation destinations could damage trust.
- The experience could collapse into a promotional banner if orientation and decision support are too thin.

### Constraints

- The feature must be supported by current, approved program data and imagery.
- It cannot become a carousel of equal-weight events or a duplicate calendar.
- Dining's filters, categories, and restaurant actions remain primary.
- Program and venue identities must remain distinct.
- No urgency, availability, or exclusivity claim may exceed the source of truth.

## Concept 4

### Concept ID

CON-04

### Concept Name

Savor Rituals

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer
- REC-02 — Make venue and program relationships reciprocal
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Recurring Programming Recognition Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests may assume Savor Society is limited to occasional special events and miss its year-round recurring experiences, especially when a dated event does not match their visit.

### Component Pattern

Recurring Experience Story

### Content Strategy

Governed recurring-program content explains the cadence and character of ongoing experiences such as tastings, afternoon tea, Latin night, or sunset wine programming without reproducing a full schedule. Each story identifies its host venue, recurring nature, planning information when confirmed, and whether the next step is program detail, venue detail, reservation, or future updates.

### Placement Strategy

This experience belongs in the continuation phase of Dining discovery, after guests have engaged with the venue-led purpose and may be open to reasons to revisit, extend a stay, or explore a different dining occasion. It also connects to the ongoing-experiences area of Savor Society.

### Why It Is Different

This concept is organized around cadence and repeatability rather than a one-time featured program, collective-level definition, or individual venue context. It frames Savor Society as an ongoing reason to return without becoming a loyalty program.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Increase awareness that Savor Society includes recurring culinary rituals as well as marquee collaborations and takeovers.

### Experience Description

Guests encounter Savor Society as a continuing set of hosted rituals across the resort. The experience helps them recognize the difference between recurring and date-specific programming, understand which venues host each type, and choose between learning about the experience, reserving when supported, or following future programming through the approved list proposition.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can identify repeatable experiences that may fit their trip even when no marquee event aligns with their dates.

### Expected Business Outcome

Ongoing programming gains sustained visibility and can support repeat consideration without relying entirely on short-lived event promotion.

### Benefits

- Communicates the year-round nature of Savor Society.
- Reduces dependence on a single dated event for awareness.
- Supports multiple host venues while preserving the collective identity.
- Gives list signup a relevant role when immediate details or reservations are unavailable.
- Differentiates recurring programming from standard venue service.

### Risks

- Cadence language may become inaccurate if recurring programs pause or change.
- The experience could resemble an event listing if too many programs are surfaced.
- Guests may infer guaranteed availability without clear operational context.
- Recurring content may compete with standard venue hours or restaurant reservations.

### Constraints

- Recurrence, schedule, status, and reservation details must have authoritative ownership.
- The experience cannot function as a loyalty proposition or calendar duplicate.
- Venue participation cannot be implied beyond confirmed programming.
- Program actions remain distinct from standard venue reservations.
- Inactive or paused recurring experiences must not appear current.

## Concept 5

### Concept ID

CON-05

### Concept Name

Choose Your Culinary Path

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Guided Decision Support Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests face several culinary intents—find a restaurant, explore special programming, reserve a specific event, or follow future experiences—but the current ecosystem does not explicitly distinguish those paths.

### Component Pattern

Intent Guide

### Content Strategy

Short, precise orientation distinguishes venue discovery from Savor Society programming. Action language reflects actual intent: continue comparing restaurants, understand the collective, view a confirmed program, reserve a specific event, or join for future updates. Supporting copy explains commitment and destination without generic booking language.

### Placement Strategy

This experience belongs at a journey transition where guests have enough Dining context to recognize different next-step intents. It supplements rather than precedes or replaces the page's primary venue discovery.

### Why It Is Different

This concept organizes the experience around guest intent and action choice. Other concepts lead with storytelling, venue relationships, current programming, recurrence, or cross-page context; this one makes the decision structure itself the central experience.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Protect restaurant discovery while making program exploration and conversion paths legible as separate choices.

### Experience Description

Guests retain the default venue-led Dining path. When they reach the decision-support experience, the distinctions among restaurant exploration, Savor Society discovery, event reservation, and future-interest signup are made explicit. Each continuation requires the level of context appropriate to its commitment, and no path implies that all programs are bookable.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can choose the culinary path that matches their current intent and understand where each action leads.

### Expected Business Outcome

Restaurant, event, and list actions receive more qualified traffic while generic CTA ambiguity and destination surprise are reduced.

### Benefits

- Directly addresses competing action intents.
- Preserves restaurant discovery as the default path.
- Supports guests at different planning stages.
- Gives non-bookable programming and list signup a clear but separate role.
- Can clarify hotel booking as unrelated to the dining-specific decision moment.

### Risks

- Too many choices could increase cognitive load.
- The experience could feel transactional if orientation is too thin.
- Unverified list benefits or unavailable event actions could create false expectations.
- Guests with simple restaurant intent may not need additional decision support.

### Constraints

- Restaurant discovery and restaurant reservations remain primary and continuously available.
- Action labels must distinguish detail, restaurant reservation, event reservation, signup, and hotel booking.
- No unsupported membership, priority, exclusivity, or inventory claims may be introduced.
- The concept cannot require personalization or new operational routing.
- Each action must have a valid, governed destination.

## Concept 6

### Concept ID

CON-06

### Concept Name

Connected Culinary Context

### Recommendation Alignment

- REC-02 — Make venue and program relationships reciprocal
- REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Experience Concept

Cross-Page Journey Experience

### User Problem Solved

Guests moving among Dining, Savor Society, venue details, and reservation destinations can lose the relationship between the collective, the program, and the host venue.

### Component Pattern

Contextual Journey Bridge

### Content Strategy

A shared relationship vocabulary identifies Savor Society as the umbrella, the program type or experience, the host venue, timing or cadence when confirmed, and the intent of each onward action. Each surface provides only the context needed for that stage and defers deeper content to its canonical source.

### Placement Strategy

This experience spans transition points among Dining, Savor Society, participating venue pages, and governed reservation destinations. Its role is continuity across surfaces rather than a new standalone destination or a single inserted feature.

### Why It Is Different

This concept treats the journey between pages as the product experience. Unlike the venue relationship concept, which enriches participating-venue evaluation, this direction focuses on preserving context and action meaning across the entire multi-page path.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Create reciprocal, understandable movement between venue-led discovery and program-led discovery without duplicating content.

### Experience Description

Guests can begin with a venue, a Savor Society program, or a specific reservation intent. As they move across surfaces, the collective-program-venue relationship remains explicit. Venue details remain distinct from program details, and restaurant, event, list, and hotel actions retain their own intent. Returning to the prior discovery context remains conceptually supported.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests maintain orientation across surfaces and can move between a program and its host venue without mistaking one destination or action for another.

### Expected Business Outcome

Dining and Savor Society reinforce one another across the resort ecosystem while canonical content ownership and conversion-path distinctions remain intact.

### Benefits

- Addresses the current one-way content relationship.
- Supports multiple guest entry points without a new landing page.
- Reduces the need to duplicate program and venue content.
- Preserves canonical destinations for Dining, Savor Society, and reservations.
- Applies shared taxonomy across the journey.

### Risks

- Inconsistent naming or stale links could make continuity worse.
- Cross-surface ownership may be fragmented.
- Reservation destinations may not preserve source context.
- The experience may be difficult to validate without a production component and navigation inventory.

### Constraints

- Canonical venue, program, and reservation destinations must be confirmed.
- Shared relationship language must be governed across surfaces.
- The concept cannot create a standalone journey page or duplicate event inventory.
- Restaurant and event reservations must remain distinct.
- Back paths, accessibility, and mobile continuity require later validation without assuming new technical capabilities.
