# Recommendation

## Executive Summary

### Top Recommendation

Position Savor Society as the culinary-programming layer of the Dining experience, not as another restaurant, dining category, or competing primary journey. Dining should remain venue-led and reservation-oriented; Savor Society should broaden that journey by making time-based, recurring, and collaboration-led experiences discoverable through their clear relationship to participating venues.

### Supporting Evidence

- Savor Society has no observed body presence on Dining and appears only under the sibling Experiences navigation category.
- Dining and Savor Society share numerous venues, including Kusa Nori, Stubborn Seed, Wally's, ¡VIVA!, Carversteak, Streetbird, and Allē Lounge on 66.
- The current relationship is one-way: Savor Society identifies host venues, but Dining does not expose related Savor Society programming.
- Dining's established utility depends on filters, category scanning, venue evaluation, and restaurant-reservation actions.
- Savor Society introduces different guest decisions: understand the program, identify timing and venue, reserve an event, explore details, or join a list.
- The press page provides stronger program definition and taxonomy than the guest-facing page, while programming freshness and ownership remain unresolved.

### Why It Matters

This direction addresses the stakeholder request while protecting the behavior that already gives Dining value. It connects culinary programming to demonstrated dining intent, clarifies that Savor Society spans venues rather than functioning as one, and creates a strategic basis for keeping restaurant, event, membership, and hotel actions distinct.

### Priority Order Of All Recommendations

1. REC-01 — Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer.
2. REC-02 — Make venue and program relationships reciprocal.
3. REC-03 — Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent.
4. REC-04 — Establish governed, reusable programming foundations.

## Recommendation Direction 1

### Name

Position Savor Society as Dining's programming layer

### Recommendation ID

REC-01

### Summary

Treat Savor Society as the cross-venue expression of what is happening in food and beverage, while keeping Dining centered on where to eat and drink. Its strategic role on Dining is to expand discovery from stable venues into timely and recurring culinary experiences without becoming a peer restaurant category or replacing the canonical Savor Society page.

### Problem Being Solved

Savor Society is absent from the Dining body even though its subject matter is highly relevant to Dining visitors. At the same time, Dining's filters, venue hierarchy, and restaurant-reservation flow are established primary tasks that could be disrupted by competing programming content.

### Opportunities Addressed

- Opportunity 1 — Dining-to-programming discoverability
- Opportunity 2 — Dining submenu relationship clarity
- Opportunity 4 — Restaurant discovery continuity

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests understand that Resorts World offers culinary programming beyond its venue directory and can recognize Savor Society as the relevant umbrella without losing the primary restaurant-discovery path.

### Expected Business Outcome

Savor Society gains qualified visibility among dining-interested guests while venue discovery, venue evaluation, and restaurant reservations remain intact.

### Assumptions

- Dining visitors have meaningful secondary interest in chef, cocktail, and recurring culinary programming.
- The Dining page remains the canonical venue-discovery surface.
- The Savor Society page remains the canonical program-discovery surface.
- Savor Society remains classified as a collective or platform rather than a restaurant or standalone venue.

### Dependencies

- Alignment on the information-architecture relationship between Dining and Experiences.
- Baseline understanding of Dining's primary guest tasks and high-value actions.
- Confirmation of the current Dining hierarchy across desktop and mobile.
- A production component inventory and content-capacity review before concept generation.

### Quality Standards

- Restaurant discovery remains the dominant journey and retains clear access to filters, categories, venue details, and reservations.
- Savor Society is oriented before any program action is presented.
- Hierarchy distinguishes a cross-venue collective from restaurant categories and individual venues.
- Commercial emphasis remains restrained, specific, and hospitality-led.
- Future concepts avoid equal-weight card density, duplicate page-level arrival treatment, and multiple competing primary actions.
- Typography and imagery clarify the program's dining relevance without implying unconfirmed venue or event relationships.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Dining-to-programming discoverability and hierarchy.

#### Design Freedom

Medium

#### Must Preserve

- Dining remains venue-led.
- Existing restaurant filters and reservation paths remain discoverable.
- Savor Society remains a cross-venue program with a canonical destination.
- The distinction between stable venue inventory and changing programming remains clear.
- Mobile scanning and semantic heading order remain intact.

#### Must Avoid

- Treating Savor Society as a restaurant, venue, or ordinary dining category.
- Replacing or obscuring restaurant discovery.
- Duplicating the full Savor Society page within Dining.
- Inventing events, dates, offers, availability, or membership benefits.
- Creating a second page-level hero that competes with Dining's arrival content.

## Recommendation Direction 2

### Name

Make venue and program relationships reciprocal

### Recommendation ID

REC-02

### Summary

Establish a coherent relationship between Savor Society programs and their participating Dining venues across both journeys. Guests should be able to understand which venue hosts an experience, what kind of program it is, and how venue-led discovery differs from program-led discovery.

### Problem Being Solved

Savor Society currently names host venues, but Dining does not identify related programming. The relationship is therefore one-way, and the program's breadth across the resort remains implicit.

### Opportunities Addressed

- Opportunity 3 — Venue-to-program relationship visibility
- Opportunity 5 — Culinary programming orientation and findability
- Opportunity 9 — Cross-page journey continuity

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can move between a venue and its relevant program context without losing orientation, and they can distinguish a dated event, recurring experience, collaboration, and host venue.

### Expected Business Outcome

Venue demand and program demand reinforce one another, supporting deeper resort exploration and clearer recognition of Savor Society's cross-venue value.

### Assumptions

- Host venue is a meaningful decision attribute for Savor Society guests.
- Venue-program mappings can be maintained accurately.
- Cross-page continuity can be supported without duplicating content ownership.
- Canonical destinations exist or can be defined for relevant venues and programs.

### Dependencies

- An authoritative mapping of programs, program types, venues, timing, and destinations.
- Shared naming and taxonomy across Dining, Savor Society, press, and venue pages.
- Defined ownership for reciprocal relationships and expired content.
- Verification of breadcrumb, contextual-linking, and back-navigation capabilities before concept generation.

### Quality Standards

- Program, venue, date or cadence, and action intent remain scannable and semantically distinct.
- The primary identity of each surface remains clear: venue discovery on Dining and program discovery on Savor Society.
- Relationships are accurate, current, and reversible where canonical destinations exist.
- Editorial language does not obscure operational details.
- Imagery preserves venue and experience identity and does not imply an inaccurate host relationship.
- Future concepts use documented pattern roles by guest need rather than visual resemblance.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Venue-to-program orientation and cross-page continuity.

#### Design Freedom

Medium

#### Must Preserve

- Clear host-venue identity.
- Clear Savor Society umbrella identity.
- Distinction between dated, recurring, and editorial programming.
- Existing venue detail and reservation paths.
- A coherent return path between program and venue contexts.

#### Must Avoid

- Implying continuous participation when a venue hosts only limited programming.
- Creating duplicate or conflicting program records.
- Using partner, chef, mixologist, or venue imagery without confirmed rights and relevance.
- Flattening all programs and venues into equal-weight cards.
- Conflating an event reservation with a standard restaurant reservation.

## Recommendation Direction 3

### Name

Separate discovery, reservation, and list-signup intent

### Recommendation ID

REC-03

### Summary

Define a coherent action hierarchy across venue exploration, restaurant reservations, event reservations, program details, and future-interest signup. Each action should reflect the guest's level of context and commitment without collapsing different conversion paths into generic booking language.

### Problem Being Solved

The current ecosystem mixes generic “Learn More,” restaurant “Book Now,” event “Reserve Your Seat,” list invitation, editorial expansion, and hotel booking. Several programs also lack sufficient timing or operational detail to support a confident next action.

### Opportunities Addressed

- Opportunity 6 — Program and reservation CTA intent
- Opportunity 7 — Event reservation decision support
- Opportunity 8 — Savor Society list value and access

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests know whether they are exploring, reserving a restaurant, reserving a specific event, or joining for future programming, and they receive enough context to choose the appropriate path.

### Expected Business Outcome

Action clarity supports more qualified transitions, protects restaurant-reservation intent, strengthens event decision confidence, and gives list signup an appropriate role when immediate reservation is unavailable or premature.

### Assumptions

- Restaurant, event, list, and hotel actions represent distinct business and guest intents.
- Some Savor Society content will remain informational or pre-bookable.
- The list proposition can be operationally fulfilled as described.
- Sufficient event information can be maintained when reservation actions are available.

### Dependencies

- Confirmation of SevenRooms destinations and handoff behavior.
- Review of the JavaScript-dependent list experience, consent, accessibility, and confirmation states.
- Definition of minimum decision-support content for reservable programming.
- Alignment on the role and prominence of global hotel booking within dining and program contexts.

### Quality Standards

- CTA language distinguishes detail, restaurant reservation, event reservation, signup, and hotel booking.
- A primary action appears only after sufficient orientation and operational context.
- Multiple primary actions do not compete within the same decision moment.
- Price, date, venue, availability, and constraints remain adjacent to the action when relevant.
- Signup claims are specific, approved, and supported by actual fulfillment.
- Conversion uses relevance and confidence rather than artificial urgency.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

CTA hierarchy and conversion-path continuity.

#### Design Freedom

Low

#### Must Preserve

- Existing restaurant-reservation access.
- Clear distinction between restaurant and event reservations.
- Savor Society's editorial and discovery role before conversion.
- List signup as a different commitment from a reservation.
- Global hotel booking as a separate resort action.

#### Must Avoid

- Generic action language that hides destination or commitment.
- Multiple competing primary CTAs.
- Reservation actions without adequate date, venue, or availability context.
- Unsupported priority, exclusivity, urgency, or membership claims.
- Assuming all programs are immediately bookable.

## Recommendation Direction 4

### Name

Establish governed, reusable programming foundations

### Recommendation ID

REC-04

### Summary

Treat freshness, taxonomy, source authority, asset rights, and reusable pattern alignment as strategic prerequisites for broader Savor Society exposure. The press page can inform authoritative positioning, but guest-facing content must remain governed for planning and conversion needs rather than inherit press structure by default.

### Problem Being Solved

Programming content changes more frequently than the Dining venue directory and includes operationally sensitive dates, schedules, pricing, status, partners, talent, imagery, and reservation links. No canonical data source, ownership model, event URL inventory, or production component inventory was supplied.

### Opportunities Addressed

- Opportunity 10 — Press context as governed program evidence
- Opportunity 11 — Reusable component-role alignment
- Opportunity 12 — Programming content governance and freshness

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests encounter current, credible, and consistent program information across Dining, Savor Society, venue pages, and reservation destinations.

### Expected Business Outcome

The organization can expand program visibility with lower risk of stale dates, conflicting claims, broken destinations, inaccurate venue relationships, or ungoverned partner and imagery use.

### Assumptions

- Broader Dining exposure will increase the maintenance and trust impact of programming content.
- Press content is authoritative context but not automatically guest-ready content.
- Reusable design-system roles can support the strategic needs once production feasibility is confirmed.
- Cross-functional ownership is available for recurring governance.

### Dependencies

- Named owners and approvers for program, venue, press, reservation, legal, and asset content.
- A canonical source for program status, dates, venues, price, cadence, and destinations.
- Rules for publication, updates, expiration, sold-out, postponed, cancelled, and no-current-programming states.
- Rights confirmation for chef, mixologist, partner, press, venue, and food imagery.
- A production component inventory and accessibility review before concept generation.

### Quality Standards

- Guest-facing claims trace to an approved source and remain current.
- Press language, quotes, credentials, and imagery are used only with appropriate governance and guest relevance.
- Event status and operational details remain visible rather than hidden by editorial styling.
- Reusable pattern selection follows journey role and avoids unnecessary one-off components.
- Exact type, spacing, color, and responsive values are not invented where the design system is silent.
- Empty, expired, unavailable, and error states preserve trust and journey continuity.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Content governance, freshness, and reusable pattern readiness.

#### Design Freedom

Low

#### Must Preserve

- A single authoritative program identity and taxonomy.
- Current dates, status, venue relationships, and reservation destinations.
- Approved claims, talent credentials, and imagery rights.
- Existing design-system hierarchy, accessibility, and hospitality principles.
- The distinction between press context and guest planning content.

#### Must Avoid

- Copying press content into guest surfaces without governance.
- Inventing program inventory, dates, availability, benefits, or assets.
- Displaying stale or expired programming as current.
- Creating a pilot-only component when an established role can meet the need.
- Relying on imagery or atmospheric styling to carry essential operational information.
