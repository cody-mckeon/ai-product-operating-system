# Vendor Handoff

## Executive Summary

Resorts World Las Vegas intends to introduce Savor Society within the existing Dining page so guests can discover culinary programming while continuing to use Dining primarily to find and reserve restaurants and lounges.

The enhancement exists because Savor Society currently appears under Experiences but has no body-level presence on Dining, despite being a year-round culinary and cocktail collective hosted across participating resort venues.

The approved direction is a restrained editorial orientation experience: explain the collective, make its relationship to Dining venues understandable, and provide one clear continuation to the canonical Savor Society experience. It must not behave like a campaign banner, event calendar, restaurant category, or second landing page.

Success means guests understand what Savor Society is and why it is relevant without losing the Dining page's venue-discovery and restaurant-reservation journey. The agency has flexibility in execution where exact components, copy, responsive behavior, and production assets have not been formally approved.

## Objective

### Confirmed Objective

**Approved:** Increase Savor Society awareness within the Dining journey while preserving Dining as the primary venue-discovery and restaurant-reservation experience. Savor Society remains a cross-venue culinary and cocktail collective, not a restaurant, venue, or Dining category.

Source: `recommendation.md` — REC-01; `concept-selection.md` — Unified Design Direction; `design-review.md` — Experience Translation.

**Confirmed fact:** The current Dining page is organized around filters, Dining categories, venue summaries, venue-detail links, and restaurant reservations. Savor Society does not currently appear in the retrieved Dining body.

## Concept Summary

### Guest Need

Guests browsing Dining need to know that Resorts World also offers chef collaborations, mixology takeovers, and recurring culinary experiences through Savor Society. They also need to understand that Savor Society spans participating venues rather than functioning as one venue or category.

### Guest-Facing Behavior

**Approved:** The experience provides collective-level orientation first, participating-venue evidence second, and one program-discovery continuation after enough context has been established.

Source: `concept-selection.md` — Unified Design Direction; `design-review.md` — Information Hierarchy Strategy and Conversion Strategy.

### Expected Guest Outcome

Guests can identify Savor Society, understand its connection to Dining venues, and choose whether to explore the canonical Savor Society experience without losing restaurant discovery or confusing a program action with a restaurant reservation.

Source: `concept-selection.md` — Expected Guest Outcome; `design-review.md` — Experience Translation.

### Relationship To The Existing Journey

Dining remains canonical for venue discovery and standard restaurant reservations. Savor Society remains canonical for collective and program discovery. Event reservations and list signup remain within the Savor Society or program context, not the inserted Dining orientation.

Source: `recommendation.md` — REC-01 and REC-03; `design-review.md` — CTA And Reservation Continuity Review.

## Experience Intent

### Confirmed Design Intent

Guests should notice:

1. The Dining page remains a restaurant and lounge discovery experience.
2. Savor Society is a culinary and cocktail collective.
3. The collective operates through participating venues and multiple program types.
4. One clear continuation leads to deeper Savor Society discovery.

Source: `design-review.md` — Information Hierarchy Strategy.

The enhancement should feel editorial rather than promotional, helpful rather than urgent, and integrated rather than campaign-like. Orientation must precede action. Participating venues should validate the relationship without becoming another venue grid or event inventory.

Source: `design-review.md` — Core Experience Pattern, Trust Strategy, and Visual Principles.

The module should support a calm progression back into restaurant discovery. It must not compete with the Dining hero or make Savor Society appear more important than the page's primary task.

Source: `design-review.md` — Placement Recommendation and DR-01 Recommendations.

### Implementation Recommendations

**Recommended:** The latest screenshot's two-part editorial composition is a useful reference, but the agency may adapt composition to the current RWLV component and responsive system if the approved hierarchy and behavior remain intact.

**Recommended:** Use one focal image and one button-style CTA. Other venue relationships, if interactive, should remain visually secondary.

**Recommended:** On mobile, use a sequential order that preserves orientation, visual evidence, venue evidence, the program CTA, and a clear return to the first Dining category. Exact ordering of image and venue evidence remains open to responsive design validation.

**Recommended:** Avoid fixed dimensions or screenshot-derived spacing. Match the existing Dining page's approved production tokens and conventions.

Sources: `design-review.md` — Mobile Behavior Review and Visual Principles; `figma-prompts.md` — DR-01.
