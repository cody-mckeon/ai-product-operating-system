## Observed Strengths

* The Entertainment page has a clear event-discovery foundation with hero merchandising, event listing controls, event cards, and multiple commercial CTAs.
* The page supports several venue and event types, including Resorts World Theatre, Zouk Nightclub, Ayu Dayclub, The Stage at Zouk, and Rose Rooftop event labels.
* The event card pattern consistently exposes artist/event name, venue label, date, imagery, and ticket/table/detail CTAs.
* The Resorts World Live page has a focused lineup pattern with `Just Announced` events and `Secure Tickets` CTAs.
* The Resorts World Live page includes stay-oriented merchandising through a `Concert Weekend Stay` section and `Book Your Stay` CTA.
* The global navigation already exposes Resorts World Live under Entertainment and Experiences, creating an existing path into the Resorts World Live page.
* FAQ content is robust and covers ticketing, policies, directions, food and beverage, and accessibility.
* Footer social links exist across reviewed pages, establishing an existing global social-link pattern.

## Observed Weaknesses

* Resorts World Live is present in global navigation and on its own Experiences page, but it is not represented as a dedicated body section on the Entertainment page.
* The Stage at Zouk appears as an event-card venue label, but not as a page-level heading or venue-orientation section.
* The current on-page anchor navigation includes Resorts World Theatre, Ayu Dayclub, and Zouk Nightclub, but not Resorts World Live or The Stage at Zouk.
* The Entertainment page body hierarchy places FAQ before the Venues section, so support content interrupts the transition from Theatre content to broader venue discovery.
* Venue discovery is concentrated near the lower page and currently highlights Ayu Dayclub and Zouk Nightclub, with no observed body-level section for The Stage at Zouk.
* The Entertainment page is primarily ticket/table oriented; stay-package exposure is more visible on the Resorts World Live page than during Entertainment page event discovery.
* Venue-level social integrations were not observed in the body of the Entertainment page or Resorts World Live page.
* AEG appears in multiple contexts: logo/intro placement near the top of the Entertainment page and an FAQ cancellation/postponement answer. The stakeholder input separates AEG content removal from AEG logo placement, creating a content-governance distinction to clarify.

## Opportunity 1

### Name

Entertainment page hierarchy clarity

### Observation

The Entertainment page combines hero content, AEG intro content, event discovery, Theatre content, Theatre Amenities, FAQ, Venues, booking utilities, footer content, social links, and chat support in one long page.

### Evidence

The current-state review documents the observed order as hero carousel, on-page anchor navigation, AEG logo/intro, event carousel/listing content, filters, event listing, Resorts World Theatre, Theatre Amenities, FAQ, Venues, booking widget/footer, footer social links, and chat.

### User Impact

Guests may need to scan a long sequence of mixed content types to understand what entertainment options exist, how venues relate to each other, and where to act.

### Business Impact

Mixed hierarchy can dilute attention across entertainment discovery, venue merchandising, ticket conversion, stay booking, and support content.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to clarify the Entertainment page hierarchy so venue discovery, event discovery, support content, and commercial actions are easier to distinguish.

### Dependencies

* Confirm desired page hierarchy across Marketing, Entertainment, Product, and Web/CMS stakeholders.
* Confirm which modules are required on the Entertainment page versus better suited to destination or support pages.
* Confirm whether observed screenshots and live-page source represent the current production state.

## Opportunity 2

Resorts World Live positioning

### Observation

### Evidence

Current-state evidence shows Resorts World Live in the Entertainment and Experiences navigation. The Entertainment page body includes Resorts World Theatre, Theatre Amenities, FAQ, and Venues sections, but no dedicated Resorts World Live body section after The Theatre section.

### Business Impact

Limited body-level visibility may constrain awareness, event discovery, and ticket engagement for Resorts World Live programming.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to improve Resorts World Live visibility within the Entertainment page experience while preserving its relationship to The Stage at Zouk.

### Dependencies

* Confirm the approved relationship between Resorts World Live, The Stage at Zouk, and any related programming.
* Confirm whether Resorts World Live should be merchandised as a program, venue experience, event series, or page destination.
* Confirm available copy, imagery, lineup data, and CMS fields.

## Opportunity 5

### Name

Event discovery decision support

### Observation

The Entertainment page event listing exposes event imagery, artist/event name, venue label, date, and CTAs. It includes date range, category, artist, and view-mode controls.

### Evidence

Current-state and component-inventory evidence identify event listing controls, event cards, venue badges, date rows, and CTA variants including `Get Tickets`, `Buy Tickets`, `Tables`, `Learn More`, and `Book Now`.

### User Impact

Guests can browse many events, but may need additional context to compare venue type, event type, ticket path, table path, and stay relevance when deciding what to do next.

### Business Impact

Event discovery is a major conversion surface. Any ambiguity in event type, venue context, or next action may affect ticketing, table reservations, and cross-sell engagement.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to improve decision support within event discovery by making the relationship between events, venues, actions, and guest intent easier to evaluate.

### Dependencies

* Confirm available event metadata from the CMS or event feed.
* Confirm which CTA labels and destinations are controlled internally versus by third-party ticketing or venue partners.
* Confirm whether guests commonly enter with a date-first, artist-first, venue-first, or experience-first intent.

## Opportunity 10

### Name

AEG content and logo governance

### Observation

The Entertainment page currently includes a visible AEG logo placement near the top intro area, AEG-associated intro context, and FAQ copy that references AEG Presents in cancellation/postponement language.

### Evidence

Current-state evidence identifies the AEG logo before the Entertainment intro and notes an FAQ cancellation/postponement answer referencing AEG Presents. The pilot brief includes both `Remove AEG content from Entertainment page` and `Add AEG logo placement on Entertainment page`.

### User Impact

Guests may encounter partner branding and partner references without clear distinction between sponsor/partner logo placement, page content ownership, and operational ticketing language.

### Business Impact

Partner branding has business, legal, and relationship implications. Unclear distinction between removable content and required logo placement can create content-maintenance risk.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to clarify the role of AEG on the Entertainment page as a content, logo, partner, and policy-reference concern.

### Dependencies

* Confirm contractual, legal, and brand requirements for AEG logo placement.
* Confirm which AEG references are content, legal/policy copy, partner attribution, or required operational language.
* Confirm approval workflow for partner-brand changes.
