## Recommended Direction

The highest-value strategic direction is to clarify the Entertainment page hierarchy around Resorts World Live and The Stage at Zouk first, because it directly addresses the pilot question and most stakeholder requests. Ticket conversion, stay-package exposure, venue-level social context, and AEG governance should be pursued as supporting directions that strengthen the page once the hierarchy is clear.

## REC-01

### Name

Clarify Entertainment Hierarchy Around Resorts World Live

### Summary

Prioritize a clearer Entertainment page hierarchy that makes Resorts World Live and The Stage at Zouk understandable within the broader entertainment experience. This direction addresses the most central gap in the current state: Resorts World Live exists in navigation and on its own page, while The Stage at Zouk appears in event cards, but the Entertainment page does not clearly connect them in the body hierarchy.

### Problem Being Solved

Guests can encounter Resorts World Live, The Stage at Zouk, Resorts World Theatre, Zouk Nightclub, and Ayu Dayclub across navigation, event cards, and page sections without a consistent hierarchy that explains their relationship.

### Opportunities Addressed

* Opportunity 1: Entertainment page hierarchy clarity
* Opportunity 2: Resorts World Live positioning
* Opportunity 3: The Stage at Zouk representation
* Opportunity 4: Navigation and anchor visibility
* Opportunity 6: Venue discovery continuity
* Opportunity 9: FAQ placement and journey flow

### Supporting Rationale

The current-state evidence shows that Resorts World Live appears in global navigation and has a standalone Experiences page, but no dedicated Entertainment page body section. The Stage at Zouk appears as an event-card venue label, not as a page-level heading. The on-page anchor navigation lists Resorts World Theatre, Ayu Dayclub, and Zouk Nightclub, but not Resorts World Live or The Stage at Zouk. FAQ content currently sits between Theatre Amenities and Venues, interrupting venue-discovery continuity.

### Expected User Impact

Guests should better understand what Resorts World Live is, how The Stage at Zouk relates to it, and where that experience fits among Theatre, dayclub, nightclub, and event-listing content.

### Expected Business Impact

Clearer hierarchy should improve discoverability for Resorts World Live programming, reduce content ambiguity, and create a stronger foundation for ticket conversion and future merchandising work.

### Risks

* Resorts World Live could be misread as a separate venue if taxonomy is not governed carefully.
* Page hierarchy changes may create tension between partner branding, venue merchandising, and event discovery priorities.
* FAQ movement may reduce visibility for support content if support needs are not understood.

### Assumptions

* Resorts World Live should remain associated with The Stage at Zouk.
* The Entertainment page is an appropriate place to clarify this relationship.
* Venue discovery is a meaningful part of the Entertainment page journey.

### Dependencies

* Approved taxonomy for Resorts World Live and The Stage at Zouk.
* Confirmed content ownership for venue and programming descriptions.
* Approved section inventory for the Entertainment page.
* Understanding of FAQ engagement and support requirements.
* Agreement on anchor structure across desktop and mobile.

### Quality Standards

* Resorts World Live must not be presented as a separate venue from The Stage at Zouk.
* Entertainment remains the primary browsing context.
* The relationship between event listings, venue sections, and page anchors must be clear.
* FAQ remains accessible and findable.
* Hierarchy changes must preserve ticket and venue discovery paths.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Entertainment page hierarchy, Resorts World Live positioning, The Stage at Zouk representation, FAQ placement, and anchor/navigation structure.

#### Design Freedom

Medium

#### Must Preserve

* Resorts World Live is associated with The Stage at Zouk.
* The Stage at Zouk is the user-facing venue context for Resorts World Live.
* Existing event discovery and ticket paths remain visible.
* Resorts World Theatre, Ayu Dayclub, and Zouk Nightclub remain discoverable.

## REC-02

### Name

Strengthen Event Discovery And Commercial Action Clarity

### Summary

Prioritize clearer continuity between event discovery and commercial actions. The current Entertainment page has a strong event-card foundation, but guests see multiple CTA types across tickets, tables, learn-more paths, venue bookings, stay bookings, and complimentary tickets.

### Problem Being Solved

Guests may need to infer the difference between ticket purchase, table reservations, event details, venue booking, stay booking, and other action paths.

### Opportunities Addressed

* Opportunity 5: Event discovery decision support
* Opportunity 7: Ticket conversion path clarity
* Opportunity 12: Measurement visibility across discovery and conversion

### Supporting Rationale

Current-state evidence identifies event listing controls, event cards, venue labels, dates, and CTA variants including `Get Tickets`, `Buy Tickets`, `Secure Tickets`, `Tables`, `Learn More`, `Book Now`, `Book Your Stay`, `Complimentary Tickets`, and `View More Shows`. Destination examples include third-party ticketing, table, and booking-engine paths. Measurement evidence for these handoffs was not visible.

### Expected User Impact

Guests should have stronger confidence about which action fits their intent and what kind of next step each action represents.

### Expected Business Impact

Greater action clarity should support ticket conversion, table reservation continuity, outbound handoff quality, and future measurement of event-discovery effectiveness.

### Risks

* CTA governance may vary by event type, venue partner, or third-party platform.
* Clarifying actions without downstream data may improve perceived clarity but leave conversion impact unknown.
* Ticket and table partners may constrain label or destination changes.

### Assumptions

* Event discovery is a primary conversion surface for the Entertainment page.
* CTA clarity can influence guest confidence and continuation.
* Ticket, table, and booking destinations can be audited or governed.

### Dependencies

* Inventory of CTA labels, destinations, and ownership by event and venue type.
* Analytics or reporting access for outbound ticket, table, and booking handoffs.
* Agreement on primary and secondary commercial actions by venue type.
* Confirmation of third-party partner constraints.

### Quality Standards

* Commercial actions must remain easy to scan.
* Ticket purchase paths must not be diluted.
* Venue and event context must remain visible at the decision point.
* Future concepts should distinguish intent without adding unnecessary friction.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Event discovery, CTA hierarchy, ticket conversion support, table reservation clarity, and measurement visibility.

#### Design Freedom

Medium

#### Must Preserve

* Event cards remain the core event-discovery surface.
* Ticket conversion remains a primary action.
* Third-party ticket and table handoffs remain discoverable.
* Date and venue context remain visible near actions.

## REC-03

### Name

Connect Entertainment Discovery To Stay-Package Potential

### Summary

Explore the strategic role of stay-package cross-promotion within Entertainment discovery, especially where event dates and Resorts World Live intent are already present. This direction should be pursued after the hierarchy is clarified so package exposure supports, rather than competes with, ticket conversion.

### Problem Being Solved

Guests who discover entertainment on the Entertainment page may not see stay-related relevance connected to their event interest, while the Resorts World Live page already contains `Concert Weekend Stay` merchandising.

### Opportunities Addressed

* Opportunity 8: Stay-package cross-promotion visibility
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 5: Event discovery decision support
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 7: Ticket conversion path clarity

### Supporting Rationale

The Resorts World Live page includes a `Concert Weekend Stay` module with a `Book Your Stay` CTA. The Entertainment page includes global booking entry points and a lower booking widget, but observed event discovery is primarily ticket/table/detail oriented. Entertainment interest creates a plausible room-night and package cross-sell context, but package eligibility and operational rules were not available in the evidence.

### Expected User Impact

Guests with travel intent should have a clearer path to understand when an entertainment event can be part of a broader resort stay.

### Expected Business Impact

This direction could increase awareness of stay opportunities, strengthen entertainment-to-room consideration, and support future package merchandising strategy.

### Risks

* Stay messaging could distract from ticket purchase if introduced without clear hierarchy.
* Package availability may vary by event, date, venue, tower, or operational constraints.
* Booking-engine routing and package inventory may be difficult to maintain.

### Assumptions

* Some entertainment guests have or can develop stay intent.
* Resorts World Live programming has meaningful stay-package potential.
* Stay-related messaging can coexist with ticket conversion when governed carefully.

### Dependencies

* Package eligibility rules by event and date.
* Booking-engine routing and package availability confirmation.
* Ownership model for package content and updates.
* Alignment between Entertainment, Hotel Revenue, Marketing, Product, and Web/CMS teams.

### Quality Standards

* Ticket conversion remains protected.
* Stay-package visibility must be accurate and operationally maintainable.
* Package messaging must be date-aware where eligibility depends on dates.
* Future concepts should clarify stay relevance without implying unavailable offers.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Entertainment-to-stay journey, stay-package cross-promotion, Resorts World Live event-to-room consideration.

#### Design Freedom

Low

#### Must Preserve

* Ticket purchase remains the primary event action where appropriate.
* Package exposure must not imply universal availability.
* Existing booking paths remain discoverable.
* Operational accuracy matters more than promotional volume.

## REC-04

### Name

Use Venue-Level Social Context To Support Discovery Confidence

### Summary

Evaluate venue-level social context as a discovery-confidence layer for entertainment venues. This should remain secondary to hierarchy and conversion clarity, but it aligns with stakeholder interest in social integrations for all entertainment venues.

### Problem Being Solved

Guests evaluating entertainment venues may not see current social proof, venue atmosphere, artist context, or venue-specific social channels during page exploration.

### Opportunities Addressed

* Opportunity 11: Venue-level social integration visibility
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 6: Venue discovery continuity
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 3: The Stage at Zouk representation

### Supporting Rationale

Current-state evidence shows global footer social links on both reviewed pages, but no body-level venue-specific social feeds, social CTAs, artist social modules, or venue social modules. Venue sections for Ayu Dayclub and Zouk Nightclub include booking CTAs but no observed social context.

### Expected User Impact

Guests should gain better confidence about venue energy, recency, and relevance when evaluating entertainment options.

### Expected Business Impact

Venue-level social context could support engagement, venue consideration, and confidence for ticket/table decisions, especially for entertainment experiences where atmosphere matters.

### Risks

* Social content introduces brand-safety, moderation, rights, performance, and maintenance concerns.
* Social content can distract from commercial CTAs if not governed carefully.
* Venue-specific channels may vary in quality, cadence, and ownership.

### Assumptions

* Social proof can support entertainment decision confidence.
* Official venue social channels exist and can be governed.
* Social integrations are intended to support discovery and confidence, not replace ticket paths.

### Dependencies

* Confirm official social channels for each venue.
* Confirm moderation, rights, brand-safety, and performance requirements.
* Confirm whether social context should be global, venue-level, artist-level, or event-level.
* Confirm ownership for ongoing social content governance.

### Quality Standards

* Social context must support, not compete with, event and venue actions.
* Social content must be brand-safe and maintainable.
* Venue identity must remain clear.
* Future concepts must account for content availability differences across venues.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Venue-level social context, social proof, venue discovery confidence, and entertainment engagement.

#### Design Freedom

Medium

#### Must Preserve

* Ticket and booking paths remain prominent.
* Social content is clearly connected to a venue or entertainment context.
* Footer social links remain available as global account access.
* Brand-safety and rights constraints are respected.

## REC-05

### Name

Clarify AEG Content, Logo, And Policy Governance

### Summary

Resolve the difference between AEG content removal and AEG logo placement before concept generation. This is a governance direction rather than a guest-experience centerpiece, but it is necessary because stakeholder requests explicitly separate removing AEG content from preserving or adding logo placement.

### Problem Being Solved

The current Entertainment page contains a visible AEG logo, AEG-associated intro context, and FAQ copy that references AEG Presents. It is not yet clear which AEG references are removable content, required logo placement, partner attribution, or legal/operational policy language.

### Opportunities Addressed

* Opportunity 10: AEG content and logo governance
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 1: Entertainment page hierarchy clarity
* Supporting relationship to Opportunity 9: FAQ placement and journey flow

### Supporting Rationale

Current-state evidence identifies AEG logo placement near the top of the Entertainment page and an FAQ cancellation/postponement answer referencing AEG Presents. The pilot brief includes both `Remove AEG content from Entertainment page` and `Add AEG logo placement on Entertainment page`, indicating a need for clear governance before future content or hierarchy decisions.

### Expected User Impact

Guests should encounter partner branding and policy language in a way that feels clear, purposeful, and consistent with the page's entertainment context.

### Expected Business Impact

Clear governance should reduce partner-brand risk, legal/policy ambiguity, and future content-maintenance churn.

### Risks

* Contractual requirements may limit available content changes.
* Removing or moving AEG references without governance could create partner or legal issues.
* FAQ language may serve a policy purpose beyond page merchandising.

### Assumptions

* AEG-related content and AEG logo placement are governed by different requirements.
* Partner, legal, or operational stakeholders may need approval rights.
* AEG governance can be resolved before detailed concept generation.

### Dependencies

* Contractual and brand requirements for AEG placement.
* Legal or partner review of FAQ policy language.
* Content ownership for Entertainment intro and support content.
* Approval workflow for partner-brand updates.

### Quality Standards

* Partner branding must follow approved requirements.
* Policy language must remain accurate.
* AEG logo placement and AEG content should be treated as distinct governance questions.
* Future concepts should not assume all AEG references are interchangeable.

### Concept Generation Input

#### Focus Area

Partner-brand governance, AEG logo/content distinction, and policy-reference clarity.

#### Design Freedom

Low

#### Must Preserve

* Required partner obligations.
* Accurate policy and cancellation language.
* Clear distinction between logo placement, content, attribution, and legal references.

## Deferred Or Secondary Themes

Measurement visibility is included inside REC-02 because it is necessary to understand ticket conversion and CTA handoff performance. It should not become a standalone concept-generation direction until analytics access, event taxonomy, and third-party reporting constraints are confirmed.

Mobile-specific navigation remains an important evidence gap. It should be evaluated during future concept work, especially because the desktop anchor bar was observed but mobile anchor behavior was not visible in the reviewed screenshots.
