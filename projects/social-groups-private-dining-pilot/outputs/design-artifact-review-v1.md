## Approved Intent Baseline

The approved primary direction is **CON-01 — One Team, Many Occasions**. It
should create a dedicated RWLV-framed orientation and inquiry entry for social
planning. The stable idea is one continuous guest planning relationship
supported by relevant specialists, not one proven department, person, form,
CRM route, or fulfillment system. Sources: `outputs/concept-selection.md` and
`outputs/design-review.md`.

The approved hierarchy is:

1. Unified planning relationship.
2. Guest occasion or planning need.
3. Confirmed capabilities and RWLV property breadth.
4. Approved team, event, property, and operational proof.
5. Confirmed package and event-window relevance.
6. Qualification context and truthful inquiry.

DR-01 and DR-02 are approved for exploration. DR-03, DR-04, and DR-05 remain
conditional on approved team scope and proof, package facts and governance,
and a verified inquiry model. Services, venues, packages, proof, and seasons
must not become equal top-level systems. Source: `outputs/design-review.md`.

The page should be recognizably RWLV: warm, composed, cinematic, editorial,
operationally clear, and restrained. RWLV global navigation, footer, property
identity, and downstream systems must be preserved rather than redesigned.
Zouk may inform human expertise, event evidence, portfolio confidence, and
operational specificity, but not the page-wide visual skin. Sources:
`outputs/design-review.md`, `outputs/visual-audit.md`, and
`outputs/figma-prompts.md`.

All unresolved claims require explicit, honest placeholders. The exploration
must not invent or approve package facts, capability scope, team members,
roles, proof, response expectations, CTA labels or destinations, routing, or a
universal form. Required mobile exploration must preserve the hierarchy and
must not merely stack desktop modules. Source: `outputs/figma-prompts.md`.

## What Works

- The seven captured sections follow the approved page-level sequence from
  orientation through occasion recognition, property breadth, proof, packages,
  planning context, and an inquiry transition.
- The artifact keeps the primary page role as a dedicated RWLV landing page
  rather than turning it into a Zouk microsite, venue catalog, long RFP, or
  temporary F1/NFR campaign.
- The hero makes the unified planning proposition the dominant opening idea and
  keeps the primary planning action visibly unresolved.
- The artifact preserves the strategic distinction between occasion discovery,
  package relevance, qualification context, and inquiry rather than collapsing
  them into one form.
- Package cards visibly disclose that content, imagery, availability, pricing,
  and terms are pending. The area is subordinate to the proposition and
  property context.
- The final section explicitly distinguishes planning, hotel/stay booking,
  table reservation, and support paths, and it states that routing is
  conditional.
- The page avoids a dark Zouk clone, a testimonial wall, invented named staff,
  client logos, price-led offers, countdowns, and urgency treatment.
- The large-scale editorial rhythm and generous whitespace support an initial
  exploration of a calm, premium page rather than a dense operational form.

## What Drifted

- The occasion/need layer became a five-tile image grid with similarly weighted
  destinations. This is the equal-card-wall behavior explicitly rejected by
  Design Review and the Figma prompt.
- Several conditional or unknown statements are rendered as real consumer copy
  and labeled as approved or verified. The most serious examples are
  `Verified specialists`, repeated `[VERIFIED CAPABILITY]` labels, the claim
  that one specialist coordinates across the full portfolio, and detailed
  capability statements that the source material says still require team
  validation.
- The v1 header appears to invent or reinterpret global navigation and actions,
  including `STAY`, `DINE`, `PLAY`, `EXPERIENCE`, `EVENTS`, `Reservations`, and
  `Plan an Event`, instead of demonstrating preservation of the observed RWLV
  global and Meetings & Events context.
- The artifact uses actual-looking occasion labels, sublabels, imagery, and the
  Bond Street mark beneath `[APPROVED OCCASION / NEED LABEL]` annotations. The
  taxonomy, asset approval, category relationships, and cross-brand rights are
  unresolved, so the composition visually launders exploratory material into
  something that can read as approved.
- Team and proof treatment uses generic human imagery and an event image while
  labeling their roles as approved evidence. Those images do not establish the
  real planning organization or approved cross-property proof.
- The packages module uses a real-looking `INQUIRE` action on each card even
  though package routes and final CTA labels are unresolved. It also does not
  show the required no-approved-package fallback state.
- Planning-context statements imply factual routing and operations, including
  routing to the right specialist, program-dependent event windows, property
  scope, and advance coordination. These may be plausible, but they are not
  confirmed as publishable facts by the approved evidence.
- The visual language relies heavily on bold sans-serif and italic display type,
  black rules, very pale imagery, and stark white fields. It does not yet carry
  enough of the observed RWLV light display hierarchy, warm neutral texture,
  restrained red action cues, or clear property-specific visual signals.
- No mobile artifact was supplied despite the explicit requirement to create a
  mobile exploration alongside desktop.

## What Must Change

1. Remove every false approval or verification signal. Replace unconfirmed
   consumer-facing claims, labels, capabilities, roles, proof, and operating
   statements with unmistakable role-based placeholders or confirmed source
   content only.
2. Rework the occasion/need section from an equal five-card grid into a curated
   editorial orientation with intentional hierarchy. Preserve access to both
   emotional occasions and practical needs without implying packages or equal
   business priority.
3. Restore the existing RWLV global frame as observed or mark it as a preserved
   existing component. Do not invent global navigation, reservations behavior,
   or a `Plan an Event` destination.
4. Remove or neutralize unapproved imagery and marks. Each image role must state
   source and approval status; the Bond Street mark and generic people/event
   images must not function as approved taxonomy, team, or proof evidence.
5. Recast property breadth as a governed capability-responsibility study.
   Specific capability statements must remain pending until team scope,
   relationships, availability, and ownership are verified.
6. Keep team and proof conditional. Remove `Verified specialists` and other
   factual team-scope claims; use collective or role-based placeholders and
   keep each proof placeholder adjacent to the exact claim it would validate.
7. Remove the package-card `INQUIRE` labels, show action label and destination
   as pending, and include the required evergreen no-approved-package fallback.
8. Convert planning-context copy into requirement-level placeholders where it
   currently implies a confirmed field, routing rule, service boundary, or
   operational fact.
9. Produce mobile evidence for the full sequence. It must preserve hierarchy,
   avoid horizontal-only essential navigation and repeated image-card length,
   keep proof source and package constraints together, and distinguish planning
   from booking, reservation, and support.
10. Improve observable contrast and legibility, particularly text over the hero,
    occasion cards, team/proof imagery, planning-context proof, and the final
    image-led CTA. Keep essential claims and actions outside busy imagery.

## What Should Not Be Changed

- Keep CON-01 as the experience spine and DR-02 as the discovery model.
- Keep the dedicated RWLV page role and the top-to-bottom journey sequence.
- Keep one continuous planning relationship as the dominant opening question,
  while preserving its unconfirmed operating scope.
- Keep services, venues, packages, and proof subordinate to the planning
  relationship and occasion/need orientation.
- Keep packages after orientation, breadth, and proof rather than moving offers
  to the page opening.
- Keep qualification separate from inquiry and avoid building a full form.
- Keep planning, hotel/stay booking, table reservation, and support as distinct
  action purposes until routing is confirmed.
- Keep the route-neutral planning-action placeholder and the explicit statement
  that no universal form is assumed.
- Keep the restrained, non-urgent package posture and the avoidance of a Zouk
  clone, nightlife saturation, venue directory, or campaign page.
- Keep the generous pacing and chapter-level composition, refining it toward
  RWLV evidence rather than replacing the page with a new concept.

## Detailed Findings

### Finding 1 — Unconfirmed Claims Presented As Verified

- **Dimension:** Content Truthfulness; Strategy Alignment; Content Governance
- **Observation:** The artifact labels specialists and capabilities as verified
  while the supporting copy or source evidence says team scope remains pending.
- **Expected state:** Unconfirmed team scope, capability relationships, and
  operational facts remain explicit placeholders and cannot read as approved
  consumer content.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `team-proof.png` uses `Verified specialists.
  Visible across every occasion.`; `property-breadth.png` repeats `[VERIFIED
  CAPABILITY]` above six detailed statements while also noting that
  capabilities are pending team validation.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Team Framing
  Review, Content Governance, and Content Status Inputs;
  `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 1 constraints.
- **Impact:** The artifact could overstate the unified operating model and allow
  stakeholders or a vendor to treat provisional services as confirmed.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Remove verified/approved labels and replace the
  claims with role-based pending states until an owner validates scope and
  language.

### Finding 2 — Occasion Discovery Became An Equal Card Wall

- **Dimension:** Approved Hierarchy; Component And Pattern Fit; Guest Clarity
- **Observation:** Five similarly styled image tiles make provisional occasion
  categories look like equal primary destinations.
- **Expected state:** A small, curated occasion/need orientation with editorial
  variation, progressive detail, and visible distinction between emotional
  occasions and practical planning needs.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `group.png` uses one large card and four
  similarly weighted cards with titles and service-like sublabels.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Occasion / Need
  Discovery Review and Not Recommended; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 1
  and Prompt 2 guardrails.
- **Impact:** The section creates taxonomy overload, implies equal business
  priority, and risks turning exploratory combinations into fixed bundles.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Preserve occasion-first discovery but replace the
  grid with a deliberately ranked editorial composition.

### Finding 3 — Existing Global Navigation Is Not Reliably Preserved

- **Dimension:** Page Role; Strategy Alignment; Inquiry Honesty
- **Observation:** The header presents a complete navigation and two actions
  that are not established as the existing preserved RWLV component in the
  supplied evidence.
- **Expected state:** Preserve the actual RWLV global navigation, footer, and
  downstream systems without redesigning or assigning new action behavior.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `Hero.png` and `group.png` show `STAY`, `DINE`,
  `PLAY`, `EXPERIENCE`, `EVENTS`, `Reservations`, and `Plan an Event`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 1 Existing
  Components To Preserve and Exploration Constraint;
  `outputs/current-state.md` — Existing Page Hierarchy and CTA inventory.
- **Impact:** The design implies approved site architecture and action
  destinations outside the pilot's authority.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Use an accurate preserved-component reference or a
  clearly labeled frame placeholder with no invented destination behavior.

### Finding 4 — Placeholder Imagery And Marks Read As Approved Evidence

- **Dimension:** Placeholder Handling; Proof Integrity; Brand Fit
- **Observation:** Actual-looking images and a Bond Street mark are placed under
  approved-evidence annotations without source, rights, or relevance
  verification.
- **Expected state:** Asset-role placeholders remain unmistakably provisional,
  with internal source, rights, approval, and freshness annotations. Generic
  imagery cannot stand in for team or operational proof.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `group.png`, `property-breadth.png`,
  `team-proof.png`, and `planning-context.png` use polished imagery alongside
  labels such as `[APPROVED RWLV PROPERTY IMAGE]`, `[APPROVED TEAM OR ROLE
  EVIDENCE]`, and `[APPROVED EVENT PROOF]`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Proof And Trust
  Review; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 1 and Prompt 3 constraints;
  `outputs/visual-audit.md` — Evidence Gaps.
- **Impact:** Reviewers may infer approval, rights, representative proof, or
  cross-property capability that does not exist in the evidence set.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Replace with neutral asset-role placeholders or
  identify a real source and approval status outside the consumer composition.

### Finding 5 — Package Treatment Implies An Inquiry Route And Omits Fallback

- **Dimension:** Package And Seasonal Treatment; Inquiry Honesty; CTA Clarity
- **Observation:** Each provisional package card contains `INQUIRE`, and the
  section shows only a three-offer state.
- **Expected state:** Action label and destination remain pending; the study
  includes both a governed future-package state and an evergreen
  no-approved-package fallback with custom planning kept distinct.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `packages-relevance.png` shows three package
  cards with `INQUIRE` actions and no fallback state.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Package And
  Seasonal Content Review; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 4 constraints
  and required module elements.
- **Impact:** The artifact suggests that package inquiry behavior and a
  publishable package set exist, weakening operational honesty.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Return actions to explicit pending placeholders and
  add the no-package state without inventing offer facts.

### Finding 6 — Planning Context Contains Unverified Operational Statements

- **Dimension:** Inquiry Honesty; Content Truthfulness; Content Governance Risks
- **Observation:** The orientation layer uses plausible but unapproved routing,
  scope, timing, and coordination statements as consumer copy.
- **Expected state:** Qualification topics remain requirement-level
  placeholders. No field, route, service boundary, lead time, or specialist
  handoff is represented as factual until confirmed.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `planning-context.png` includes `May help route
  your inquiry to the right specialist`, `Event windows vary by venue and
  program`, `Scope varies across RWLV properties and dining locations`, and
  `Some programs require advance coordination`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Inquiry And CTA
  Review; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 5 constraints.
- **Impact:** Guests and downstream teams could treat hypotheses about routing
  and fulfillment as established operating rules.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Replace with neutral pending topics or cite an
  approved operational source before using consumer-facing language.

### Finding 7 — Required Mobile Exploration Is Missing

- **Dimension:** Mobile Behavior; Accessibility Risks
- **Observation:** The v1 evidence contains desktop crops only.
- **Expected state:** A mobile exploration accompanies desktop and preserves
  hierarchy, context, proof relationships, status, constraints, and distinct
  action intent.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** No mobile image exists under
  `screenshots/figma-make/v1/`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Mobile Behavior
  Review; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 1 Mobile Behavior.
- **Impact:** Essential responsive behavior, reading order, density, action
  distinction, and proof/package integrity cannot be assessed.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Supply named mobile captures for the complete
  sequence before the next gate review.

### Finding 8 — Contrast And Text-Over-Image Legibility Are At Risk

- **Dimension:** Accessibility Risks; Visual Tone; Guest Clarity
- **Observation:** Several sections use small or medium black text over pale,
  busy, or gradient-covered imagery with uncertain contrast. Operational
  annotations are especially small.
- **Expected state:** Essential claims, labels, placeholders, and actions remain
  readable with sufficient contrast and are not dependent on image position.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `Hero.png`, `group.png`, `team-proof.png`,
  `planning-context.png`, and `planning-next-step.png` use text over imagery;
  some text visually merges with image detail or light overlays.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Accessibility;
  `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — visual and mobile requirements.
- **Impact:** Guests may struggle to read the hierarchy and status language;
  static screenshots cannot establish compliance.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Move essential text to stable surfaces or strengthen
  verified contrast, then provide reviewable desktop and mobile states.

### Finding 9 — RWLV Visual Frame Is Under-Specified

- **Dimension:** Brand Fit; Visual Tone
- **Observation:** The artifact is restrained and editorial, but its dominant
  bold/italic typography, black linework, stark white fields, and washed imagery
  feel like a generic editorial concept more than the observed RWLV system.
- **Expected state:** RWLV remains unmistakable through accurate property
  context, light/refined hierarchy, warm neutral fields, restrained red action
  cues, varied editorial rhythm, and real environmental imagery.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** All v1 captures, particularly `Hero.png`,
  `team-proof.png`, and `planning-next-step.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — RWLV / Zouk Visual
  Synthesis Review; `outputs/visual-audit.md` — Typography, Color, Imagery, and
  Page Rhythm Observations.
- **Impact:** The page may feel polished but insufficiently grounded in the
  approved property frame.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Required disposition:** Refine the existing composition toward observed
  RWLV behavior without inventing exact tokens or rebuilding the concept.

### Finding 10 — Successful Route-Neutral Separation Should Be Preserved

- **Dimension:** Inquiry Honesty; CTA Clarity
- **Observation:** The closing section distinguishes a pending planning action
  from hotel/stay booking, table reservation, and support, and explicitly says
  routing is conditional.
- **Expected state:** Maintain distinct action purposes without selecting a
  universal form, owner, destination, or response promise.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `planning-next-step.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — CTA Hierarchy;
  `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — Prompt 5.
- **Impact:** This protects guest clarity and operational truth during revision.
- **Severity:** Observation
- **Required disposition:** Preserve the separation and pending-state language;
  improve contrast without adding final behavior.

## Unable To Verify

- **Mobile hierarchy and behavior — Blocking for progression:** No mobile
  artifact was supplied. The next review must include the complete mobile
  sequence.
- **Exact page continuity — Non-blocking for revision:** Separate section crops
  imply a sequence, but no single full-page capture or Figma node was supplied.
- **Component reuse and editability — Non-blocking for revision:** No Figma node
  or design context was supplied, so component structure and inspectability
  cannot be confirmed.
- **Navigation and CTA destinations — Blocking for final approval:** Static
  screenshots cannot establish where `Plan an Event`, `Reservations`,
  `INQUIRE`, anchors, or planning actions go.
- **Proof and image rights — Blocking for stakeholder approval and handoff:** No
  source, rights, approval, freshness, or representative-coverage record was
  supplied for v1 imagery or the Bond Street mark.
- **Team and capability truth — Blocking for stakeholder approval and handoff:**
  Participating roles, scope, cross-team relationships, and fulfillment
  boundaries remain unconfirmed.
- **Package facts and seasonality — Blocking for populated package approval:**
  Names, value, inclusions, exclusions, pricing posture, terms, availability,
  imagery, August 4 meaning/year, route, inactive state, and ownership remain
  unresolved.
- **Inquiry behavior and states — Blocking for final CTA approval:** No routing,
  fields, privacy behavior, validation, error, success, unavailable, handoff,
  or response-expectation evidence was supplied.
- **Accessibility compliance — Non-verifiable from screenshots:** Focus,
  semantics, keyboard behavior, alternative text, reduced motion, error
  association, and measured contrast require design context and testing.

## Readiness Decision

**Status: Major Revision.**

The artifact is a useful first structural exploration. It demonstrates the
approved page role, sequence, package subordination, qualification/inquiry
separation, and route-neutral closing posture. Those strengths mean the work
does not need to return to Design Review and the selected concept should not be
replaced.

It is not ready for stakeholder review as an approvable direction or for Vendor
Handoff. Major findings remain in content truthfulness, placeholder handling,
occasion hierarchy, navigation preservation, proof integrity, package action
honesty, mobile coverage, and observable accessibility. Revise the v1 artifact
within the approved direction, capture desktop and mobile v2 evidence, and run
the Design Artifact Review Agent again.
