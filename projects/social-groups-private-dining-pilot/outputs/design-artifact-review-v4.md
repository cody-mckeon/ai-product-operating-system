## Approved Intent Baseline

The approved primary concept remains **CON-01 — One Team, Many Occasions**.
The page should function as a dedicated RWLV-framed orientation and inquiry
entry for social occasions. It should make one continuous planning relationship
understandable without claiming that one person, department, form, owner, CRM
route, or fulfillment system handles every need. Sources:
`outputs/concept-selection.md`, `outputs/recommendation.md`, and
`outputs/design-review.md`.

The approved seven-stage journey is:

1. Arrival and unified planning orientation.
2. Occasion or practical-need recognition.
3. Governed capability and RWLV property breadth.
4. Conditional team, event, property, and operational proof.
5. Conditional packages and current relevance.
6. Qualification orientation or planning guide.
7. Inquiry continuity into distinct downstream purposes.

Orientation and occasion/need recognition are primary. Capability, proof,
packages, qualification, and inquiry are supporting layers. Packages must
remain after orientation and trust. The page must not become a venue directory,
service catalog, package campaign, Zouk clone, Meetings & Events reskin, or
fixed itinerary. Sources: `outputs/design-review.md` and
`outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md`.

For Stakeholder Concept Review, the main composition should use readable
directional copy rather than large bracketed placeholders. Unresolved facts
must remain honest through small, legible annotations outside or near the
affected section. Directional copy must not assert package, proof, team,
capability, availability, inquiry, routing, ownership, or response facts that
have not been approved. Source: `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md`.

The visual direction should be recognizably RWLV: warm, refined, editorial,
premium, hospitality-led, and readable. Photography should establish place and
human energy; red should be restrained and purposeful; approved typography
behavior should provide editorial character. Zouk may influence human
expertise and proof behavior, not page-wide nightlife styling. Sources:
`outputs/design-review.md`, `outputs/visual-audit.md`, and
`outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md`.

## What Works

- V4 is substantially more stakeholder-readable than v3. Stages 1, 2, 3, 5,
  and 6 use recognizable headlines and supporting copy instead of making large
  content-role placeholders the main experience.
- The seven intended stages remain identifiable and appear in the approved
  order across the numbered captures.
- Arrival and occasion/need recognition remain the dominant opening
  responsibilities. Packages, qualification, and inquiry remain later in the
  journey.
- The occasion section no longer behaves like the v2 six-card destination
  wall. It uses one visual lead and three subordinate directional pathways.
- The package section does not invent a package, price, inclusion, date,
  availability, or active offer. Future active-state responsibilities are
  clearly separated from the main concept.
- Team and proof content does not invent people, departments, testimonials,
  client names, event outcomes, or property credentials. Evidence roles remain
  visibly conditional.
- The planning guide is presented as orientation rather than an interactive
  form. No fields, controls, submission behavior, or technical routing are
  depicted.
- The artifact avoids Zouk cloning, nightlife dominance, neon/glow excess,
  fake urgency, package-card walls, and overt casino clichés.
- Most annotations are plainly written and clarify what remains directional,
  especially in the occasion, capability, package, and planning-guide sections.
- No broken image, exposed alternative text, filename, or browser loading error
  is visible in the supplied v4 captures.

## What Drifted

- The inquiry section is not readable enough for stakeholder evaluation. Its
  headline, support copy, and alternate-path labels are rendered in extremely
  pale text on a white surface.
- Inquiry copy states that planning inquiry, hotel reservations, table
  reservations, and property support “must not share a form, route, owner, or
  confirmation behavior.” Approved intent requires those purposes to remain
  distinct and routing to remain unresolved; it does not establish that no
  future operational element may be shared.
- No mobile artifact was supplied despite explicit v4 requirements for a
  complete mobile sequence.
- Only isolated section captures were supplied. The complete desktop page,
  relative stage pacing, transitions, global RWLV header/footer context, and
  overall journey continuity cannot be inspected.
- Planning Support names room blocks, suite coordination, arrival experiences,
  spa access, and optional programming in the main composition. Planning Guide
  similarly describes preferred dates, event windows, group composition, room
  blocks, suite hospitality, event meals, and in-suite hospitality. The modal
  language and annotations reduce risk, but these specifics still read more
  like a proposed capability and qualification set than a neutral stakeholder
  concept.
- Team & Proof remains dominated by internal evidence-role and approval
  language. It is honest, but it still reads closer to a content-governance
  board than a stakeholder-facing trust-layer concept.
- Package and proof sections use large areas of internal review language and
  empty space. The information is accurate, but the concept's intended guest
  experience is less legible than its governance model.
- RWLV specificity is limited. The artifact uses some warm imagery and
  editorial spacing, but no complete property frame, restrained red action
  system, visible serif accent, or clearly approved RWLV environmental image is
  demonstrated in the supplied captures.
- Several annotations are small and low-emphasis enough that they may be hard
  to read in normal stakeholder presentation conditions, particularly in the
  hero.

## What Must Change

1. Supply a complete mobile v5 sequence showing all seven stages in the
   approved order. Preserve hierarchy, proof relationships, package status,
   action distinction, annotation legibility, and a deliberate mobile reading
   order.
2. Rebuild the inquiry section on a stable, high-contrast surface. Make the
   directional headline, support copy, primary action, alternate purposes, and
   annotation readable at review size.
3. Replace the assertion that inquiry purposes “must not share” a form, route,
   owner, or confirmation behavior. State only that the purposes are distinct
   and that final labels, destinations, ownership, routing, and handoffs remain
   unresolved.
4. Reduce the specificity of unconfirmed capability and qualification copy in
   Planning Support and Planning Guide. Retain readable directional language,
   but avoid implying that room blocks, suite coordination, spa access,
   programming, event-window logic, or in-suite hospitality are confirmed
   services or final qualification topics.
5. Refine Team & Proof and Packages so stakeholders can understand the intended
   guest experience before reading governance notes. Keep annotations and
   evidence responsibilities, but make them secondary rather than the dominant
   visual content.
6. Supply one complete desktop page capture with actual locked RWLV global
   components when authoritative references are available, or clearly
   secondary preserved-context bands when they are not.
7. Strengthen RWLV specificity using approved references: warm property
   context, selective editorial serif treatment, purposeful restrained red,
   and readable environmental imagery. Do not invent tokens or use unapproved
   assets as proof.
8. Increase annotation legibility where necessary without converting notes
   into primary page content or returning to bracketed-placeholder dominance.

## What Should Not Be Changed

- Keep CON-01 and the dedicated RWLV landing-page role.
- Keep the seven-stage journey and its current order.
- Keep arrival and occasion/need recognition as the primary responsibilities.
- Keep packages, proof, qualification, and inquiry subordinate.
- Keep the editorial occasion treatment rather than returning to a six-card
  grid.
- Keep the no-active-package posture and distinct future active-state
  responsibilities.
- Keep team, event, property, and operational proof conditional and free of
  invented evidence.
- Keep the planning guide non-interactive and explicitly directional.
- Keep directional copy in the main composition; do not revert every unresolved
  item to a large bracketed placeholder.
- Keep governance annotations near the affected sections, but refine their
  hierarchy and legibility.
- Keep the non-Zouk, non-nightlife, non-campaign, non-urgent posture.
- Keep the Bond Street mark and invented global controls removed.

## Detailed Findings

### Finding 1 — Required Mobile Evidence Is Missing

- **Dimension:** Mobile Behavior; Approved Hierarchy; Accessibility Risks
- **Observation:** The v4 folder contains seven desktop-width section captures
  and no mobile frame or sequence.
- **Expected state:** Stakeholder Concept Review evidence should include a
  complete mobile concept showing all seven stages, deliberate reflow, readable
  annotations, preserved proof adjacency, package fallback, and distinct action
  purposes.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** No mobile file exists under
  `screenshots/figma-make/v4/`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Mobile Behavior
  Review; `outputs/figma-prompts.md` — mobile requirements;
  `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` — Mobile Requirements.
- **Impact:** Stakeholders cannot evaluate whether the concept remains coherent
  and readable on a primary guest viewport. Responsive hierarchy and several
  accessibility risks remain unknown.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Produce a named complete mobile v5 frame and detail
  captures when the complete frame is not legible enough for review.

### Finding 2 — Inquiry Continuity Is Visually Unreadable

- **Dimension:** Guest Clarity; CTA Clarity; Accessibility Risks; Visual Tone
- **Observation:** The Stage 7 headline, support copy, eyebrow, and alternate
  paths use extremely pale warm-gray text on white. The primary action is
  darker, but the section cannot be understood as a complete decision moment.
- **Expected state:** Directional inquiry content and its annotation should be
  readable on a stable, high-contrast surface without claiming tested
  compliance.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `screenshots/figma-make/v4/07-inquiry.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Inquiry And CTA
  Review and Accessibility; `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` — Required
  Revision 7 and Responsive And Accessibility Expectations.
- **Impact:** Stakeholders cannot reliably evaluate the final stage, action
  hierarchy, or relationship among planning, booking, reservation, and support.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Recompose the section with visibly legible text,
  actions, annotations, and distinct downstream purposes.

### Finding 3 — Inquiry Copy Resolves An Unapproved Operating Rule

- **Dimension:** Inquiry Honesty; Content Truthfulness; Strategy Alignment
- **Observation:** The Design Intent copy says the four downstream purposes
  “must not share a form, route, owner, or confirmation behavior.”
- **Expected state:** Keep the purposes distinct for guest understanding while
  leaving shared intake, category branching, manual triage, transparent
  handoff, ownership, and confirmation behavior unresolved.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `screenshots/figma-make/v4/07-inquiry.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/recommendation.md` — REC-02;
  `outputs/design-review.md` — Inquiry And CTA Review;
  `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` — Content, Proof, And Inquiry Guardrails.
- **Impact:** The artifact could cause stakeholders to reject or assume an
  operating constraint that has not been decided.
- **Severity:** Major
- **Required disposition:** Replace the rule with route-neutral wording that
  distinguishes guest intent without deciding the future operating model.

### Finding 4 — Directional Capability And Qualification Copy Is Too Specific

- **Dimension:** Content Truthfulness; Content Governance Risks; Package And
  Seasonal Treatment
- **Observation:** Stage 3 refers to room blocks, suite coordination, arrival
  experiences, spa access, and optional programming. Stage 6 describes event
  windows, room blocks, suite hospitality, event meal context, and in-suite
  hospitality expectations as if they are the working content and topic set.
- **Expected state:** Directional copy may make the concept readable, but it
  should demonstrate category and planning roles without implying confirmed
  availability, coordination, or final qualification requirements.
- **Evidence from the artifact:**
  `screenshots/figma-make/v4/03-planning-support.png` and
  `screenshots/figma-make/v4/06-planning-guide.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Supporting
  Portfolio Layer, Inquiry Responsibilities, and Content Governance;
  `outputs/design-revision-notes-v2.md` — Revisions 4 and 7;
  `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` — Global Content Treatment.
- **Impact:** Stakeholders may begin reviewing an implied service inventory or
  field set instead of the concept's hierarchy and planning responsibilities.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Required disposition:** Generalize the examples, retain the annotations,
  and avoid returning to large placeholders.

### Finding 5 — Governance Still Dominates Team/Proof And Package Meaning

- **Dimension:** Guest Clarity; Component And Pattern Fit; Placeholder Handling
- **Observation:** Stage 4 is primarily an evidence-role matrix with pending
  source/rights language. Stage 5 devotes substantial visual weight to future
  active-state responsibilities. Both are honest, but their intended guest
  experience is less immediate than the governance model.
- **Expected state:** For Stakeholder Concept Review, the main composition
  should first communicate the intended trust or package-fallback experience;
  annotations and content responsibilities should remain clearly secondary.
- **Evidence from the artifact:**
  `screenshots/figma-make/v4/04-team-proof.png` and
  `screenshots/figma-make/v4/05-packages.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — Trust Layer and
  Package And Seasonal Content Review; `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` —
  Global Content Treatment and Required Revisions 4–5.
- **Impact:** Stakeholders may struggle to picture the guest experience and may
  interpret the artifact as a content workshop board rather than a page
  concept.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Required disposition:** Preserve all pending states but refine hierarchy,
  composition, and annotation placement so the guest-facing concept leads.

### Finding 6 — RWLV Brand And Existing Page Context Are Under-Evidenced

- **Dimension:** Brand Fit; Page Role; Visual Tone
- **Observation:** Warm imagery and editorial spacing are present, but the
  supplied captures do not show the RWLV global frame, a restrained red action
  system, a visible serif accent, or clearly approved property-specific imagery.
  The overall system remains largely black, white, and sans-serif.
- **Expected state:** A stakeholder concept should feel recognizably RWLV
  through approved property context, warm editorial hierarchy, restrained red,
  selective serif expression, and clear integration with the existing site.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** All seven v4 captures, especially
  `01-hero.png`, `02-occasion-need.png`, and `03-planning-support.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/design-review.md` — RWLV / Zouk Visual
  Synthesis; `outputs/visual-audit.md`; `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` —
  Brand Context Block and Required Revision 8.
- **Impact:** The concept can still read as a generic editorial microsite rather
  than an RWLV page.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Required disposition:** Strengthen approved RWLV cues and show the locked
  page context without inventing navigation, tokens, or assets.

### Finding 7 — Annotation Legibility And Hierarchy Are Inconsistent

- **Dimension:** Placeholder Handling; Accessibility Risks; Content Governance
  Risks
- **Observation:** Annotations are useful and no longer dominate every section,
  but some are very small, low-emphasis, or embedded over imagery. The hero note
  is particularly easy to miss, while Stage 4 notes become primary content.
- **Expected state:** Annotations should remain secondary, consistently placed,
  readable at export size, and clearly connected to the affected section.
- **Evidence from the artifact:** `01-hero.png`, `02-occasion-need.png`,
  `04-team-proof.png`, and `05-packages.png`.
- **Source of approved intent:** `outputs/figma-revision-prompt-v4.md` — Global
  Content Treatment and Responsive And Accessibility Expectations.
- **Impact:** Reviewers may miss a limitation in one section and feel
  overwhelmed by governance language in another.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Required disposition:** Normalize annotation placement, size, contrast, and
  relationship to the main composition.
