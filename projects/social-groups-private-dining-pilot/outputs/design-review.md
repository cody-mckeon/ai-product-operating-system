# Design Review

## Executive Summary

The selected direction is approved for further exploration as a **team-led, occasion/need-led social-planning experience within an RWLV property frame**. The page should establish one continuous planning relationship first, help guests recognize the occasion or need they are bringing second, and use services, venues, packages, team evidence, and event proof as supporting context rather than competing organizing systems.

The review approves two directions without requiring new operating assumptions:

- **DR-01 — Unified Planning Proposition** as the experience spine.
- **DR-02 — Occasion / Need Discovery Layer** as the primary discovery model.

Three supporting directions are conditionally approved:

- **DR-03 — Team And Proof Credibility Layer**, pending approved team scope and representative proof.
- **DR-04 — Packages In Context Layer**, pending package facts, August 4 clarification, ownership, availability, terms, imagery, and inquiry route.
- **DR-05 — Guided Inquiry Layer**, pending a verified guest-facing entry model, qualification needs, routing, handoffs, and fallback paths.

The design should feel recognizably RWLV: warm, composed, cinematic, editorial, operationally clear, and restrained. Zouk Private Sales should influence behavior—visible expertise, immersive event evidence, connected-portfolio storytelling, operational specificity, and a consistent inquiry posture—not become the visual skin. The result must not look like a Zouk microsite, a Meetings & Events reskin, a venue catalog, an internal department page, or a temporary seasonal campaign.

The central design guardrail is operational truth. “One team” should mean continuity for the guest supported by relevant specialists. It must not imply that one person, department, form, CRM path, or fulfillment system handles every service unless stakeholders confirm that model.

## Selected Direction Summary

The primary concept is **CON-01 — One Team, Many Occasions**. Its job is to make the planning relationship—not a venue, package, department, or event window—the stable idea that holds the portfolio together.

The selected supporting layers have distinct, subordinate roles:

| Supporting concept | Role in the selected direction | Design-review boundary |
|---|---|---|
| CON-02 — Plan by Occasion | Helps guests recognize fit through the occasion or need they understand already | Must not become a dense set of equal cards or imply preassembled bundles |
| CON-05 — Proven by the Planning Team | Makes the team proposition credible with approved people, event, property, and operational evidence | Must not overrepresent Zouk/nightlife or use unapproved proof |
| CON-06 — Guided Planning Concierge | Separates inspiration, qualification, and inquiry | Must not prescribe one form, routing model, automation, or owner |
| CON-07 — Packages in Context | Gives confirmed packages and event windows visible, maintained space | Must not organize the whole page or display invented package content |

The parked concepts remain excluded as primary models:

- **CON-03 — Social Experience Portfolio:** service-led organization is better suited to internal content governance or secondary explanation than to the guest's primary entry.
- **CON-04 — Cross-Property Social Journey:** the journey metaphor may imply an itinerary, package, or level of coordination not established by current evidence.

The user problem being solved is not simply “find a venue.” It is: **understand whether one RWLV planning relationship can help with the guest's kind of social occasion, see what relevant parts of the property may support it, gain confidence through evidence, and reach an honest next step.**

## Page Role Review

The page should function as the **primary orientation and inquiry entry for social occasions across Resorts World Las Vegas**. It should explain the portfolio relationship and prepare a planning conversation; it should not replace venue details, package terms, hotel booking, restaurant reservations, resource libraries, or operational forms.

### Placement Assessment

| Surface | User context | Benefits | Risks | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated RWLV Social Groups / Private Dining landing page | Guest is exploring whether RWLV can support a social occasion and may not know a venue or department | Gives the unified proposition a clear property-wide home and supports the full inspiration-to-inquiry journey | Can overpromise unity if ownership and routing remain vague | High |
| Entry links or contextual references from RWLV Meetings & Events, Group Dining, relevant Zouk pages, and other approved discovery surfaces | Guest enters through an existing service, venue, or property path | Preserves current strengths and gives fragmented entry points a coherent next step | Inconsistent labels or cross-domain handoffs could recreate confusion | Medium |
| Existing RWLV RFP or Zouk proposal experience | Guest is ready to provide details | Can preserve operational processes after qualification | Premature placement would make the page form-led and may send guests to the wrong default route | Deferred pending routing |
| Package- or event-window-specific campaign surface | Guest is responding to a dated offer or event | Could support focused promotion when content is confirmed | Would make the permanent experience temporary and split the unified story | Low as the primary role |

### Placement Decision

- **Primary placement:** a dedicated RWLV-framed social-planning landing page.
- **Secondary placement:** coherent entry points from existing RWLV and Zouk discovery surfaces; these are continuity needs, not redesign instructions.
- **Deferred placement:** final RFP, proposal, concierge, or category-specific destination until routing is confirmed.
- **Excluded primary placement:** a Zouk-only domain experience, an expanded Meetings & Events inventory page, or a temporary F1/NFR/package campaign.

## Recommended Experience Sequence

This sequence describes journey responsibilities, not a wireframe or fixed component stack.

1. **Arrival and orientation:** Establish the social-planning purpose, RWLV property context, and continuous planning relationship. The first impression should answer who can help and what broad kind of planning relationship is available.
2. **Occasion / need recognition:** Help guests see their reason for gathering or practical planning need without requiring a venue or department choice.
3. **Portfolio interpretation:** Explain the relevant capabilities that may support those needs—dining, private events, stays or small room blocks, in-suite hospitality, wellness, photography, and other confirmed support—without presenting them as automatic combinations.
4. **Team and proof credibility:** Validate the proposition with approved specialist framing, event evidence, property settings, capabilities, and operational facts close to the claims they support.
5. **Packages and current relevance:** Give confirmed Social Groups Hospitality Packages dedicated visibility and connect fall, F1, NFR, holidays, weddings, engagements, and milestones to stable capabilities without changing the page identity.
6. **Qualification orientation:** Explain what information helps the planning team understand fit, such as occasion, group characteristics, dates or windows, stay needs, hospitality needs, and enhancements. This is a responsibility, not a prescribed form.
7. **Inquiry and continuity:** Present the clearest planning next step supported by the confirmed routing model, set accurate expectations when known, and distinguish unrelated hotel booking, table reservation, concierge, or general-contact actions.

The sequence should move from lower-density atmosphere and orientation toward more structured evidence, detail, and action. Services, venues, proof, packages, and seasons should enter when they answer a guest question; they should not appear as five equal destination sections.

## Section Strategy

### Primary Narrative Spine

The page should have two dominant responsibilities:

1. Establish the unified planning relationship.
2. Help guests recognize the occasion or need they want help planning.

These responsibilities should receive the clearest hierarchy and most generous pacing.

### Supporting Portfolio Layer

Services should explain **what may be coordinated**. Venues should demonstrate **where an experience may happen and supply atmosphere, capacity, or operational proof**. Neither should become the main navigation system.

The portfolio should be curated into a small number of understandable need relationships, with deeper details delegated to existing or future source pages. The design should avoid an exhaustive equal-weight display of every venue and offering.

### Trust Layer

Team, event, client, property, and operational proof should sit adjacent to the proposition it validates. A single detached testimonial or logo gallery is insufficient evidence of a cross-property planning model.

### Commercial Relevance Layer

Packages and event windows should be visibly important but structurally replaceable. The evergreen page must remain coherent if no package is approved, an event window closes, or seasonal content changes.

### Decision Layer

Qualification and inquiry should follow sufficient orientation and evidence. The page should not begin with a long form, a generic proposal action, or multiple unexplained contact choices.

## Team Framing Review

The one-team proposition should be introduced as **a continuous guest relationship supported by specialists across the property**. This is both more credible and more flexible than claiming that one individual or department directly executes every service.

Approved design behavior:

- Present the collective planning promise before individual people or departments.
- Explain specialist participation only at the level confirmed by owners.
- Use named people, roles, portraits, or biographies only when approved and maintainable.
- Connect team evidence to the breadth it supports rather than presenting biographies as an isolated staff directory.
- Preserve continuity through clear language about collaboration or handoff only when that operating behavior is verified.

Conditionally approved behavior:

- Zouk-style human-scale team storytelling, subject to RWLV/Zouk brand hierarchy and approved representation across the broader portfolio.
- Role-based specialist framing when individual names are unavailable, provided the roles and relationships are factual.

Not approved:

- An invented “concierge” identity or named unified department.
- A single spokesperson presented as the owner of all experiences.
- Team portraits or biographies sourced from Zouk alone and used to imply complete property-wide ownership.
- Generic hospitality imagery used as a substitute for evidence of the actual planning organization.

## Occasion / Need Discovery Review

Occasion and guest need should be the primary discovery logic because current audiences are more likely to recognize a wedding, celebration, group dinner, bachelorette gathering, event weekend, room need, or hospitality need than an internal owner.

The discovery layer should:

- Use a limited set of meaningful relationships rather than one tile per portfolio item.
- Permit an occasion to connect to more than one confirmed capability without presenting a fixed bundle.
- Preserve explicit coverage for less visually documented needs, including small room blocks, in-suite hospitality, wellness, and Athena engagement photography.
- Let guests with a practical need enter without forcing them to choose a life-event label.
- Use editorial hierarchy, selective imagery, and progressive detail instead of a generic card wall.
- Keep labels provisional until audience priorities and taxonomy are validated.

The layer should not:

- Give Weddings, Group Dining, Zouk Private Events, room blocks, F1, NFR, wellness, and every other item identical visual weight.
- Repeat the same service description under many occasions without a clear governance model.
- imply that all related services can be booked together.
- Hide operational constraints behind atmospheric occasion storytelling.

The preferred exploration is a **curated occasion/need orientation supported by editorial features and contextual links**, not an exhaustive comparison grid.

## Proof And Trust Review

Trust should be earned in four ways:

1. **Property proof:** recognizable RWLV context, real spaces, breadth, and operational credibility.
2. **Team proof:** approved people or roles with a verified relationship to the planning promise.
3. **Experience proof:** approved event imagery or examples that demonstrate relevant occasion and capability breadth.
4. **Operational proof:** capacities, features, planning considerations, service boundaries, or process expectations where confirmed.

Zouk Private Sales provides the strongest observed behavior for human expertise, client/event evidence, portfolio confidence, and concrete detail. That behavior is useful only when adapted to the broader occasion set and governed for reuse.

Proof guidance:

- Keep evidence close to the claim it supports, consistent with the Content Proof Module doctrine.
- Use real, approved RWLV-relevant imagery; do not use generated or placeholder imagery as production proof.
- Balance nightlife and private-event energy with dining, wedding, celebration, stay, wellness, and hospitality evidence where available.
- Distinguish atmospheric inspiration from factual proof.
- Provide source, approval, freshness, and accessibility ownership for testimonials, client marks, biographies, event imagery, specifications, and floor plans.
- Let unsupported areas remain explicitly under-documented rather than borrowing unrelated proof.

A proof-rich direction is conditionally approved because evidence is currently uneven. Zouk team and event assets are observed, but corresponding evidence for room blocks, in-suite hospitality, bachelorette, wellness, Athena engagement photography, F1, NFR, and the new packages is absent or incomplete.

## Package And Seasonal Content Review

Social Groups Hospitality Packages should receive a **dedicated, maintained role within the evergreen experience**, not become the page's title, primary taxonomy, or dominant proposition.

While package facts remain unconfirmed, Design Review approves only the structural responsibility:

- The future experience must have a clear place for package discovery and evaluation.
- Package content must distinguish confirmed inclusions, guest value, eligibility, dates, availability, capacities, terms, and next steps when those facts are supplied.
- A package must not be represented with invented names, pricing, imagery, benefits, availability, or calls to action.
- The page must remain complete when no package is active or approved.
- Package interest and custom-planning interest must remain distinct enough to support different routes if required.

Event-window treatment should act as a timely relevance layer:

- Fall, F1, NFR, holidays, wedding season, and engagement demand may receive emphasis only when content, timing, and ownership are confirmed.
- Each time-sensitive item needs an activation rule, owner, freshness check, and retirement or fallback rule.
- Event-window content should connect to a stable occasion, need, capability, or confirmed package.
- No seasonal module should imply inventory, availability, inclusions, or trademark permissions that have not been verified.

DR-04 remains conditional until the package owner resolves the meaning and year of August 4 and supplies publishable content.

## Inquiry And CTA Review

The page should communicate **one clear guest-facing planning intent**, not one assumed back-end system.

### Inquiry Responsibilities

- Inspiration should help guests understand fit before any high-commitment request.
- Qualification should help guests recognize what planning information matters.
- Inquiry should present the next action and destination supported by the confirmed operating model.
- Continuity should preserve the guest's expressed context through a form, category branch, triage process, or handoff where operationally possible and verified.

### CTA Hierarchy

1. **Primary action:** begin or continue a social-planning inquiry. Final label and destination remain unresolved.
2. **Secondary actions:** explore an occasion, capability, package, venue detail, or approved proof when more context is needed.
3. **Alternate transactional or support actions:** hotel booking, table reservation, Concierge, phone, email, or chat only when their purpose is clearly distinct.

The primary action may recur after meaningful orientation or proof, following the consistent posture observed in Zouk Private Sales, but repetition must not create pressure or imply a route that has not been selected.

Conditionally approved inquiry models include:

- A shared guest-facing intake with back-end routing.
- A guest-facing entry that branches to category-specific forms.
- Manual triage by a confirmed planning team.
- A clear front door followed by transparent handoff to existing RWLV, Zouk, or Concierge destinations.

No model is selected by this review. A universal form, automated routing, CRM integration, response time, owner, or success state must not be depicted as real until confirmed.

CTA clarity cannot be fully approved while current paths include RWLV RFP tabs, a Zouk overlay, a Concierge request, phone, email, chat, hotel booking, and restaurant-oriented labels with no documented shared routing.

## RWLV / Zouk Visual Synthesis Review

The visual system should use **RWLV as the governing frame and Zouk Private Sales as a behavioral storytelling reference**.

### Preserve From RWLV

- Recognizable property context and brand hierarchy.
- Warm neutral fields, restrained red action cues, and editorial clarity without inventing exact values.
- Thin or refined display hierarchy used selectively, readable body copy, and precise labels.
- Varied editorial rhythm rather than repeated equal cards.
- Environmental photography that demonstrates real spaces, place, and hospitality.
- Clear operational states and composed form behavior.

### Adapt From Zouk

- Visible human expertise and role clarity.
- A connected portfolio story before detailed inventory.
- Immersive event imagery with a legible subject and venue identity.
- Client, event, team, and operational proof near relevant claims.
- Consistent inquiry posture after enough context has been earned.
- Concrete capacities or features where they help qualification and are confirmed.

### Do Not Carry Forward

- A black/charcoal-heavy page identity as shorthand for premium private sales.
- Nightlife saturation across weddings, wellness, engagements, dining, or stay content.
- Zouk typography, CTA styling, or venue density copied as a visual template.
- RWLV Meetings & Events' conference-first or venue-catalog hierarchy.
- Generic luxury, neon casino cues, decorative glow, or atmosphere without decision value.

The design should feel like one RWLV experience with specialist energy, not RWLV and Zouk sections stitched together.

Reuse should follow component role, not visual resemblance.

| Social Proof Layer | Approved testimonials, client context, or event validation | Conditionally approved | Do not use ungoverned Zouk proof or detached logo/quote decoration |
| Venue Orientation Feature | Explain a venue's role within an occasion or capability | Approved as supporting | Must not expand into a venue directory |

No new component category is yet justified. Occasion/need discovery should first be explored through Editorial Feature, orientation, anchor, and contextual-link behaviors already present in the system. A dedicated guided-planning pattern should be considered only if existing patterns cannot express qualification clearly after routing requirements are known.

## Mobile Behavior Review

Mobile must preserve the strategic hierarchy rather than simply stack a desktop composition.

Required review behaviors:

- Keep the unified planning proposition concise enough to remain an immediate orientation.
- Present occasion/need discovery as a clear sequence with visible relationships; do not require horizontal scrolling to access essential categories.
- Avoid repeated full-height image cards that turn portfolio breadth into excessive scrolling.
- Keep portfolio context close to the occasion or need it explains.
- Ensure team identities, roles, proof sources, and operational facts remain legible without hover or layered desktop composition.
- Use images with mobile-safe subject clarity and do not place essential text over busy crops.
- Preserve semantic heading order and clear section identity when side-by-side editorial layouts collapse.
- Keep package facts, constraints, and terms together; do not separate value from eligibility during reflow.
- Keep action labels concise and distinguish planning inquiry from hotel booking, table reservation, and support.
- Treat anchor navigation, repeated inquiry access, accordions, and progressive disclosure as testable behaviors, not automatic solutions.
- Account for empty, unavailable, expired, validation, error, success, and handoff states before approving high-fidelity inquiry or package explorations.

Because no supplied screenshot set establishes mobile behavior for the current pages, mobile approval remains a required downstream review, not an inferred extension of desktop patterns.

## Accessibility And Content Governance Review

### Accessibility

- Preserve a semantic heading hierarchy and logical reading order.
- Maintain readable line lengths, sufficient contrast, visible focus, and distinguishable links and controls.
- Avoid long all-caps passages and decorative typography for operational content.
- Ensure all essential claims and actions are available outside imagery.
- Supply meaningful alternative text or appropriate decorative treatment for imagery.
- Do not rely on color, image position, motion, or selected-state styling alone to communicate meaning.
- Respect reduced-motion needs and use motion only to clarify continuity or state.
- Keep forms, qualification, errors, recovery, and success behavior understandable to keyboard and assistive-technology users.

### Content Governance

Every major layer requires a named content owner and a factual source:

- Portfolio membership, naming, relationships, and overlap rules.
- Scope of the one-team promise and specialist participation.
- Occasion/need taxonomy and priority.
- Package facts, terms, dates, availability, imagery, ownership, and expiration.
- Seasonal/event-window activation and retirement.
- Team biographies, titles, portraits, and handoff descriptions.
- Testimonials, client marks, event imagery, venue facts, capacities, and floor plans.
- Inquiry destinations, qualification fields, routing, response expectations, and fallback paths.

Content for underrepresented areas should not be inferred from adjacent Zouk or Meetings & Events material. Missing evidence must remain visible as a readiness limitation.

### Design Debt To Carry Forward

- No documented shared team or routing model.
- No confirmed package content or unambiguous August 4 timing.
- Uneven proof across portfolio areas.
- Inconsistent current CTA labels and destinations.
- No validated mobile evidence for the reviewed source pages.
- No shared taxonomy connecting occasion, need, service, venue, package, and season.
- No confirmed reuse rights or cross-brand governance for Zouk proof assets.
- No observed validation, success, error, or recovery states for current inquiry paths.

### DR-01 — Unified Planning Proposition

- **Concept alignment:** CON-01; reinforces REC-01 and the selected combined direction.

- **Strategic clarity:** High.

- **Business value:** High.

- **Cognitive load:** Low when the proposition remains singular; Medium if team, portfolio, and action compete at arrival.

- **Recommendations:** Keep the first design question singular: who helps and what relationship is being offered. Represent continuity supported by specialists, then earn the planning action through occasion relevance and proof.
