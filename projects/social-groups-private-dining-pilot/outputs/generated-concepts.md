# Generated Concepts

## CON-01 — One Team, Many Occasions

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01, REC-02, REC-03, and REC-04. This concept makes the unified planning proposition the primary experience and uses occasions, packages, proof, and conditional inquiry as supporting layers.

### Concept Summary

A team-led planning experience in which the cross-property planning relationship is the connective idea. Guests first understand that one coordinated team can help shape many kinds of social experiences, then explore occasion families and relevant capabilities without needing to understand internal departments.

### Experience Concept

The page behaves as the front door to a planning team rather than as a venue directory or a collection of disconnected offers. Team framing establishes trust; a guest-centered portfolio demonstrates breadth; property and event proof make the proposition credible; qualification and inquiry follow only after guests understand the scope.

### User Problem Solved

Guests can recognize that dining, private events, accommodations, in-suite hospitality, wellness, photography, and celebration needs may be discussed through one planning relationship, even when operational fulfillment may involve several specialists.

### Primary Guest Need

Confidence that RWLV can coordinate a social occasion with multiple needs without requiring the guest to identify the correct department first.

### Business Role

Establish a shared social-business front door while preserving the distinction between a unified guest experience and the still-unconfirmed operational routing behind it.

### Suggested Page Role

An evergreen, cross-property social-planning overview that introduces the team proposition, expresses the portfolio, provides proof, and leads into qualification and inquiry.

### Organizing Model

Team-led with guest-need and occasion-led portfolio groupings.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual team proposition followed by occasion-family chapters, coordinated-service context, proof groupings, package and event-window layers, qualification cues, and a clear planning entry point.

### Content Strategy

Explain the planning relationship before presenting categories. Describe occasions in guest language; connect each to confirmed services and property contexts; use approved event, client, venue, and team evidence to substantiate breadth. Keep department names secondary and accurate.

### Placement Strategy

Place the unified team proposition at the beginning, the occasion portfolio in the primary discovery position, proof after the breadth claim, package and seasonal relevance within the evergreen body, and qualification immediately before inquiry.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** Cross-property social possibility and one-team framing.
2. **Portfolio discovery:** Occasion families spanning dining, private events, stays, hospitality, weddings, celebrations, wellness, bachelorette, and engagement-related experiences.
3. **Coordination context:** How confirmed services and specialists can contribute to an occasion.
4. **Proof:** Approved team, event, property, and venue evidence.
5. **Current relevance:** Confirmed packages and event-window content within the permanent portfolio.
6. **Qualification:** Occasion, group, timing, accommodation, hospitality, and enhancement needs.
7. **Inquiry:** One clear guest-facing planning entry, with destination or handoff left conditional on routing decisions.

### Inquiry Strategy

Present a single planning invitation at the experience level, then qualify intent before any operational destination. The concept can support a shared intake, category-specific destinations, or team triage, but does not assume which model is available.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Give confirmed Social Groups Hospitality Packages dedicated, maintained space as one way to engage the team. Treat fall, F1, NFR, holiday, wedding, and engagement relevance as updateable context rather than as the identity of the page.

### Visual Direction

Use RWLV's property-wide editorial restraint, environmental hospitality imagery, typography, spacing, and operational clarity. Adapt Zouk's visible-team, immersive-event, portfolio, and proof behaviors without importing a nightlife-only tone or cloning its styling.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Reuse RWLV's property framing, editorial image-and-content rhythm, category labels, and structured request behavior. Adapt Zouk Private Sales' team visibility, client/event proof sequence, multi-venue credibility, and repeated inquiry posture.

### Why It Is Different

Unlike category-first concepts, the team relationship is the primary navigation idea and every portfolio area is evidence of coordinated planning.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Make a broad, fragmented portfolio understandable as one guest-facing planning proposition while retaining RWLV credibility and routing truth.

### Experience Description

The guest moves from confidence in the team, to recognition of their occasion, to evidence of property breadth, to a small set of qualifying considerations, and finally to an inquiry path whose operational destination is determined only after confirmation.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest understands who can help, what kinds of experiences can be discussed, and what information is useful for beginning a conversation.

### Expected Business Outcome

The business gains a coherent umbrella for cross-property social demand without publicly collapsing distinct fulfillment teams or channels prematurely.

### Strengths

- Most directly expresses the one-team proposition.
- Accommodates the full portfolio without giving every item equal visual weight.
- Supports evergreen and time-sensitive content simultaneously.
- Allows Zouk-style team and proof storytelling inside an RWLV frame.

### Benefits

Creates a stable umbrella for discovery, proof, qualification, and current offers while giving every portfolio area a relationship to the same planning proposition.

### Risks

- The unified promise could overstate actual team integration.
- Broad occasion families could become vague if service relationships are not documented.
- Team storytelling will feel unsupported without approved ownership, biographies, or proof.

### Constraints

Internal department boundaries must remain invisible only where the operating model supports a coherent handoff. Claims of coordination must be supportable, and no portfolio combination can imply an unconfirmed bundle.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Confirmed team and ownership model; approved occasion taxonomy; service-to-occasion mapping; inquiry routing decision; package facts and launch-year confirmation; approved team, event, client, and property proof; content ownership and update cadence.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests the recommendation's central premise in its clearest form: the planning relationship, rather than any single venue, service, or campaign, is the product guests first encounter.

## CON-02 — Plan by Occasion

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01, REC-02, and REC-03, with REC-04 supporting the property and proof treatment.

### Concept Summary

An occasion-led experience that starts with what the guest is planning—such as a wedding, engagement, celebration, milestone, bachelorette gathering, holiday event, or event-weekend stay—and then reveals the confirmed services that may support it.

### Experience Concept

Guest intent supplies the primary mental model. Each occasion path connects inspiration to relevant dining, private-event, accommodation, in-suite hospitality, wellness, photography, and planning considerations without presenting those connections as fixed packages unless they are confirmed.

### User Problem Solved

Guests do not need to translate a personal occasion into RWLV's departmental or venue structure before determining whether the property can help.

### Primary Guest Need

Quick recognition of their reason for gathering and a clear view of the planning possibilities associated with it.

### Business Role

Capture varied social demand in guest language while allowing several business areas to participate behind a common occasion frame.

### Suggested Page Role

An occasion-discovery hub that progresses from inspiration to service fit, qualification, and conditional inquiry.

### Organizing Model

Occasion-led, supported by services, settings, packages, and team proof.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual occasion selector or set of occasion narratives, each connected to capability cues, relevant proof, planning questions, and a consistent inquiry framework.

### Content Strategy

Use approved occasion categories as editorial entry points. Within each, distinguish possible services from confirmed packaged combinations. Consolidate repeated capability information and identify which evidence belongs to each occasion without duplicating whole stories.

### Placement Strategy

Introduce the one-team promise first, place occasion discovery at the center of the page, reveal services and property contexts beneath each occasion, then use shared proof and qualification before inquiry.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** Occasion recognition supported by diverse social imagery.
2. **Occasion discovery:** Guest-centered paths for confirmed priority occasions.
3. **Capability context:** Relevant dining, event, stay, suite, wellness, photography, and hospitality possibilities.
4. **Proof:** Occasion-relevant property, event, venue, and team evidence.
5. **Current relevance:** Packages and event windows associated only where confirmed.
6. **Qualification:** Occasion-specific planning facts and needs.
7. **Inquiry:** A consistent planning invitation whose routing remains conditional.

### Inquiry Strategy

Carry the selected or recognized occasion into qualification conceptually, then route according to the confirmed operating model. Do not imply that every occasion uses the same form or owner.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Place confirmed packages alongside the occasions they support and maintain a separate current-relevance layer for fall, F1, NFR, and holidays. Evergreen occasion paths remain intact when time-sensitive content changes.

### Visual Direction

Use RWLV's warm, editorial property storytelling to give distinct occasions their own atmosphere. Apply Zouk's immersive event imagery and proof density selectively, with tone changing appropriately across weddings, wellness, dining, celebrations, and event weekends.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Reuse RWLV Group Dining's setting-aware editorial behavior and RWLV's property context. Adapt Zouk's event galleries, proof, and proposal posture to occasion narratives without reproducing its visual skin.

### Why It Is Different

The guest's reason for gathering—not the team, service inventory, venue, or package—is the primary discovery structure.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Reduce destination confusion by matching the portfolio to recognizable guest intent.

### Experience Description

The guest identifies an occasion, sees the range of confirmed ways RWLV may support it, understands the role of the planning team, reviews relevant proof, and supplies information appropriate to that occasion before inquiry.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest can locate a personally relevant path quickly and understand the breadth available beyond the first service they had in mind.

### Expected Business Outcome

RWLV can expose cross-sell potential through occasion context while collecting clearer intent for routing and follow-up.

### Strengths

- Uses language likely to match guest intent.
- Makes portfolio breadth relevant rather than inventory-like.
- Creates natural homes for weddings, engagements, celebrations, and bachelorette experiences.
- Supports seasonal demand without replacing the evergreen structure.

### Benefits

Lets guests enter through familiar life moments and gives the business clearer occasion intent without exposing its internal organization.

### Risks

- Guests with multiple or ambiguous occasions may not know which path to choose.
- The same service may repeat across several paths and create governance overhead.
- Occasion categories could imply unsupported bundles or omit business-important demand.

### Constraints

Occasion categories must be validated, mutually understandable rather than perfectly exclusive, and connected only to confirmed capabilities.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Priority occasion taxonomy; audience and demand evidence; approved service mappings; occasion-specific proof; qualification requirements; routing rules; package applicability; seasonal publishing ownership.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests whether guest intent can make a complex cross-property portfolio feel immediately legible while keeping the unified team visible as the enabling relationship.

## CON-03 — Social Experience Portfolio

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01, REC-03, and REC-04, with REC-02 governing qualification and inquiry.

### Concept Summary

A service-led portfolio experience organized around planning capabilities such as gathering and dining, private-event production, stays and in-suite hospitality, and complementary wellness or commemorative experiences. Occasions appear as examples of need, not as the main structure.

### Experience Concept

The page explains the range of capabilities one planning team can coordinate across the property. It gives guests a durable understanding of what can be planned even when their occasion is unusual, multi-part, or not represented by a standard label.

### User Problem Solved

Guests with known logistical needs can understand available planning capabilities without searching several venue, department, and request pages.

### Primary Guest Need

Clarity about which practical parts of a social experience RWLV may be able to support.

### Business Role

Create a governed portfolio framework capable of absorbing new services, packages, and event-window content without rebuilding the page around each launch.

### Suggested Page Role

An evergreen capability overview and qualification bridge for social-business demand.

### Organizing Model

Service-led, with team, occasion, setting, proof, and package context.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual capability map composed of service families, use-case examples, cross-property evidence, planning dependencies, and a shared inquiry framework.

### Content Strategy

Define each capability family in plain guest language, clarify what is confirmed, show representative occasions and settings, and identify when coordination with another specialist may be involved. Keep venue lists subordinate to capability stories.

### Placement Strategy

Lead with the unified team proposition, move quickly into capability families, follow with examples of how capabilities can work across occasions, and place packages, proof, qualification, and inquiry after the guest understands fit.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** A cross-property view of a coordinated social experience.
2. **Capability discovery:** Confirmed service families and their guest value.
3. **Application:** Occasions and event contexts in which each capability may be relevant.
4. **Property proof:** Settings, events, operational details, and team evidence.
5. **Current relevance:** Confirmed hospitality packages and event-window modules.
6. **Qualification:** Needed capabilities, timing, group characteristics, and known constraints.
7. **Inquiry:** Planning entry followed by confirmed routing or handoff.

### Inquiry Strategy

Allow guests to identify one or several capability needs before inquiry. Use those signals for routing only after ownership and destination rules are confirmed.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Position packages as defined expressions of one or more capabilities. F1, NFR, fall, holidays, and wedding periods can highlight relevant capability combinations without becoming permanent taxonomy.

### Visual Direction

Use RWLV's structured editorial rhythm and environmental imagery to distinguish capability families. Bring in Zouk's operational detail, portfolio confidence, and proof behaviors where evidence supports them.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Reuse RWLV's meeting-resource clarity and Group Dining editorial sections while avoiding a meetings-led tone. Adapt Zouk venue specifications and portfolio detail as supporting evidence rather than as a catalog.

### Why It Is Different

It is organized by what the team can help coordinate, making it less dependent on a fixed list of occasions and more explicit about practical scope.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Establish a scalable, governable portfolio model that clarifies breadth without becoming a venue directory.

### Experience Description

The guest starts with a broad social ambition, identifies the capabilities needed to realize it, sees where and how RWLV has relevant credibility, and enters qualification with a clearer statement of scope.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest can assemble a mental picture of the support they need and determine whether a planning conversation is appropriate.

### Expected Business Outcome

The business receives more explicit demand signals and gains a structure that can accommodate new approved offers over time.

### Strengths

- Makes operational breadth explicit.
- Handles unconventional or multi-part occasions well.
- Provides a durable home for room blocks and in-suite hospitality.
- Can incorporate packages without making them the whole proposition.

### Benefits

Creates a maintainable capability framework that can support both simple requests and multi-service planning conversations.

### Risks

- Service terminology may reflect internal structure rather than guest language.
- The experience could feel functional instead of emotionally compelling.
- Capability families may suggest coordination that the operating model cannot yet deliver.

### Constraints

The service inventory and ownership model must be accurate, understandable, and maintained. Categories cannot be presented as combinable unless that relationship is confirmed.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Approved capability inventory; service definitions; operational owners; availability boundaries; service-to-occasion examples; proof and specifications; routing logic; package details; governance model.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests a scalable alternative to occasion taxonomy and may better serve guests who arrive with several practical needs already defined.

## CON-04 — Cross-Property Social Journey

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01, REC-03, and REC-04, with REC-02 providing the inquiry guardrails.

### Concept Summary

A cross-property journey experience that shows how one team may help coordinate different moments around a social stay: arriving, staying, gathering, dining, celebrating, restoring, and commemorating. The sequence is illustrative, not a prebuilt itinerary or promised bundle.

### Experience Concept

The property becomes a connected canvas for a multi-moment social experience. Guests understand breadth through the flow of a stay or celebration rather than through categories alone, while team and proof content demonstrate how separate moments can feel coherent.

### User Problem Solved

Guests planning more than a single meal or event can see how accommodation, hospitality, dining, entertainment, wellness, and commemorative needs relate across time.

### Primary Guest Need

A holistic view of how a group occasion could unfold across the property.

### Business Role

Reveal cross-property demand and the value of coordinated planning without defining fixed itineraries or commercially unconfirmed combinations.

### Suggested Page Role

An inspirational planning narrative that bridges property breadth to concrete qualification.

### Organizing Model

Journey-led, supported by team, occasion, service, package, and proof layers.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual sequence of experience moments, each linked to confirmed capabilities, representative property settings, evidence, and planning considerations.

### Content Strategy

Describe moments as possibilities, not promises. Show varied occasions within the same journey logic, identify optional or context-dependent services, and use evidence to distinguish credible coordination from aspirational adjacency.

### Placement Strategy

Introduce one-team planning and the cross-property canvas first, use the journey as the primary narrative body, insert proof at relevant moments, then summarize fit, current packages, qualification, and inquiry.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** The property as a connected setting for a social stay or celebration.
2. **Journey discovery:** Illustrative moments before, during, and after the central occasion.
3. **Capability context:** Confirmed services associated with each moment.
4. **Proof:** Event, setting, team, and operational evidence embedded in the journey.
5. **Current relevance:** Packages and event windows attached to appropriate moments when confirmed.
6. **Qualification:** Which moments matter, group profile, timing, lodging, hospitality, and enhancements.
7. **Inquiry:** A planning conversation initiated after scope is understood, with routing conditional.

### Inquiry Strategy

Use the guest's relevant journey moments as qualification signals. The final destination may be shared intake, specialist routing, or team triage; the concept does not determine the back-end model.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Show packages as confirmed ways to support particular moments, not as fixed end-to-end itineraries. F1 and NFR can emphasize stay and in-suite hospitality moments; fall, weddings, engagements, and holidays can emphasize other moments while the journey remains evergreen.

### Visual Direction

Favor immersive, varied environmental photography and editorial pacing that conveys movement across the resort. Preserve RWLV warmth and property legibility while adapting Zouk's event energy and proof density selectively.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Reuse RWLV's destination-scale imagery, map/property context, and editorial section variation. Adapt Zouk's progression from atmosphere to team, proof, and operational detail without reproducing a nightlife-led visual system.

### Why It Is Different

It organizes the portfolio by the temporal experience of a social stay rather than by occasion, department, service, or offer.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Demonstrate cross-property breadth and coordinated value in a way that can encourage multi-need planning.

### Experience Description

The guest follows an illustrative experience arc, recognizes relevant moments, sees which confirmed capabilities may support them, and converts that mental itinerary into qualification information for the planning team.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest can envision a connected experience and identify needs they may not have considered initially.

### Expected Business Outcome

The business can surface adjacent opportunities such as room blocks, suites, hospitality, wellness, dining, and photography without presenting an unverified bundle.

### Strengths

- Makes property breadth emotionally and practically coherent.
- Creates natural context for small room blocks and in-suite hospitality.
- Supports multi-day and event-window demand.
- Avoids a flat inventory presentation.

### Benefits

Helps guests recognize adjacent needs and helps the business communicate the value of cross-property coordination without defining a fixed itinerary.

### Risks

- An illustrative journey may be mistaken for an available package.
- Single-need guests may perceive unnecessary complexity.
- A linear sequence may not fit every occasion or planning process.

### Constraints

Every connection between moments must be described conditionally unless operationally confirmed. The journey cannot promise availability, bundling, timing, or ownership.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Validated guest journeys; service relationships; accommodation and suite-hospitality rules; event-window use cases; proof for different moments; routing logic; package scope; legal and operational review of coordination claims.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests whether RWLV's greatest differentiator—the ability to host many parts of a social experience across one property—can become the clearest expression of the unified team proposition.

## CON-05 — Proven by the Planning Team

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01, REC-02, and REC-04, with REC-03 supplying governed current relevance.

### Concept Summary

A proof-led private-sales experience in which team credibility, past-event evidence, property settings, and operational detail carry the story. The portfolio is understood through demonstrated planning outcomes rather than broad claims alone.

### Experience Concept

The page alternates between the unified planning proposition and evidence of the team's reach: approved team framing, representative social experiences, client or event proof, setting breadth, and relevant planning details. Proof helps guests move from inspiration to qualification and inquiry.

### User Problem Solved

Guests evaluating a high-consideration occasion can assess whether the planning team has credible experience and property reach before sharing details.

### Primary Guest Need

Trust that RWLV can plan and coordinate a social experience appropriate to the occasion.

### Business Role

Turn existing event, venue, and team evidence into private-sales credibility while exposing the gaps where proof or ownership is not yet established.

### Suggested Page Role

An evergreen social-planning sales narrative grounded in evidence rather than an inventory of offerings.

### Organizing Model

Proof-led and team-led, with portfolio and occasion interpretation.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual evidence sequence combining team roles, representative event stories, property and venue examples, operational facts, portfolio coverage, qualification, and inquiry.

### Content Strategy

Use only approved and attributable proof. Frame event examples around planning challenges and scope without inventing testimonials or results. Pair atmospheric imagery with concrete, governed facts and make venue evidence serve the larger team story.

### Placement Strategy

Place a concise team proposition first, introduce proof early, use portfolio coverage to interpret what the evidence demonstrates, then present current packages, qualification, and inquiry with additional proof near decision points.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** Immersive evidence of varied social experiences across RWLV.
2. **Trust:** Team remit and approved indicators of planning credibility.
3. **Proof narratives:** Representative events, settings, and operational details.
4. **Portfolio interpretation:** What confirmed needs and occasions the evidence supports.
5. **Current relevance:** Approved packages and seasonal/event-window examples.
6. **Qualification:** Guest needs considered in the planning process.
7. **Inquiry:** A clear planning invitation with routing left conditional.

### Inquiry Strategy

Use proof to build confidence before requesting planning details. Repeated inquiry cues may point to the same guest-facing invitation, but the eventual form, overlay, handoff, or destination depends on stakeholder routing decisions.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Support packages with factual content and relevant evidence once available. Seasonal and event-window proof can rotate within an evergreen evidence framework rather than dictate the page identity.

### Visual Direction

Adapt the effectiveness of Zouk Private Sales' immersive imagery, visible people, past-event evidence, and operational specificity inside RWLV's restrained typography, warm palette, property framing, and broader occasion range.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Most directly adapts Zouk Private Sales' team, event, client, portfolio, and detail behaviors. Preserves RWLV's property context, editorial restraint, category clarity, and credibility signals. FUHU-style venue details may support individual examples but do not define the page.

### Why It Is Different

Evidence, rather than taxonomy or a planning sequence, is the primary means of explaining the unified proposition.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Balance the emotional confidence of private-sales storytelling with verifiable RWLV breadth and operational clarity.

### Experience Description

The guest encounters varied event evidence, understands the team and capabilities behind it, maps that credibility to their own needs, and reaches inquiry with a more concrete basis for trust.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest feels able to evaluate fit and credibility before beginning a planning conversation.

### Expected Business Outcome

The business gains a stronger sales story for high-consideration social demand and a reusable framework for governed proof.

### Strengths

- Most directly builds on the strongest observed Zouk behavior.
- Replaces abstract breadth claims with evidence.
- Supports premium and high-consideration occasions.
- Can make a unified team feel tangible.

### Benefits

Provides a credibility-first sales narrative that can connect emotional imagery to verifiable planning and property evidence.

### Risks

- Existing evidence is uneven across the requested portfolio.
- Proof could skew toward nightlife or specific venues and distort the broader offer.
- Team or client content may require significant approval and maintenance.

### Constraints

No team member, testimonial, client, event outcome, or service result can be invented. Proof must have clear usage rights, ownership, accessibility treatment, and update governance.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Approved team framing; usable team and event imagery; permissioned client or event evidence; substantiated operational facts; proof coverage across portfolio areas; package evidence; inquiry ownership; content approval workflow.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests whether the observed strength of Zouk's private-sales storytelling can be adapted behaviorally—not copied visually—to create confidence in a broader RWLV planning proposition.

## CON-06 — Guided Planning Concierge

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01 and REC-02 are central; REC-03 and REC-04 support current relevance, property context, and trust.

### Concept Summary

A guided planning experience that helps guests move deliberately from inspiration, through a small set of qualification decisions, to the appropriate inquiry destination once routing is confirmed.

### Experience Concept

The page acts as a planning orientation tool. It introduces the unified team and portfolio, helps guests articulate occasion, group, stay, hospitality, timing, and enhancement needs, and then initiates an inquiry or handoff consistent with the actual operating model.

### User Problem Solved

Guests facing several overlapping pages and CTAs receive one understandable sequence for determining fit and beginning the right conversation.

### Primary Guest Need

Guidance about what information matters and where to begin without being forced to choose an internal department.

### Business Role

Improve intent clarity and reduce guest-facing inquiry confusion while leaving implementation and routing architecture open.

### Suggested Page Role

An evergreen inspiration-and-qualification gateway to social planning.

### Organizing Model

Guided inquiry-led, supported by a concise team and portfolio overview.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual guided planner with progressive planning questions, context at each decision, summary of recognized needs, and a conditional final handoff. This identifies experience behavior only, not a specific form or interface.

### Content Strategy

Keep early content inspirational and low commitment. Explain why each qualification topic matters, use guest language, allow multi-need intent, and avoid requesting information that no confirmed owner will use. Provide proof and package context where it aids a decision.

### Placement Strategy

Open with the team proposition and compact portfolio framing, place guided qualification at the center, supply contextual proof and current relevance during the journey, and reserve the final inquiry action for the point at which the guest understands next steps.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** One-team promise, property breadth, and representative occasions.
2. **Orientation:** What the team may help plan and what remains subject to confirmation.
3. **Qualification:** Occasion, date or window, group profile, dining/event needs, stay or room-block needs, in-suite hospitality, and optional enhancements.
4. **Context:** Relevant proof, settings, packages, or seasonal information attached to decisions.
5. **Planning summary:** A guest-readable view of expressed needs.
6. **Inquiry:** Confirmed destination, shared intake, or team handoff based on the validated routing model.

### Inquiry Strategy

Make one guest-facing planning sequence visible while explicitly allowing multiple operational outcomes. Preserve alternatives for guests who are not ready or whose need falls outside confirmed routing. Do not assume a universal back-end form, data integration, or personalization capability.

### Package / Seasonal Strategy

Surface confirmed packages or event-window information only when relevant to the needs a guest is considering. The guided framework remains evergreen when package or seasonal content is absent or retired.

### Visual Direction

Use RWLV's structured form clarity, calm spacing, and property credibility, enriched by Zouk-style team and event proof at key confidence points. Keep the experience hospitable rather than administrative.

### Reuse of Existing RWLV or Zouk Patterns

Reuse the clarity of RWLV's structured RFP behavior while changing the experience from meeting-first intake to social-planning orientation. Adapt Zouk's repeated proposal posture, team visibility, and evidence without assuming its overlay or routing model.

### Why It Is Different

The primary value is decision support and inquiry clarity, not browsing a taxonomy or consuming a linear sales narrative.

### Strategic Goal Supported

Separate inspiration, qualification, and inquiry while reducing the burden created by multiple current destinations.

### Experience Description

The guest learns the planning scope, works through relevant needs at an appropriate depth, receives contextual evidence, and reaches an honest next step shaped by confirmed ownership and routing.

### Expected Guest Outcome

The guest knows what to provide, feels prepared to inquire, and avoids guessing among RFP, proposal, concierge, phone, email, chat, and booking paths.

### Expected Business Outcome

The business can receive clearer intent and direct it more consistently once governance and routing are established.

### Strengths

- Most directly addresses current inquiry confusion.
- Distinguishes browsing from qualification and conversion.
- Can capture multi-need intent without exposing department structure.
- Creates a disciplined reason to clarify routing before implementation.

### Benefits

Makes the transition from browsing to inquiry more understandable for guests and potentially more actionable for receiving teams.

### Risks

- The experience cannot be credible until ownership and routing are resolved.
- Too much qualification could feel like a long RFP.
- Contextual behavior may imply technical capabilities that are not available.

### Constraints

The concept remains channel- and implementation-neutral. It cannot prescribe a universal form, automated routing, saved state, personalization, or integration without technical and operational confirmation.

### Required Inputs or Dependencies

Inquiry ownership; routing matrix; minimum useful qualification fields; privacy and data requirements; escalation and fallback paths; response expectations; package applicability; technical feasibility; measurement plan.

### Why This Concept Should Be Considered

It tests whether the new landing page can solve not only content fragmentation but also the guest's uncertainty about how to begin planning.

## CON-07 — Packages in Context

### Recommendation Alignment

REC-01 and REC-03 are central, with REC-02 governing inquiry and REC-04 governing brand and proof.

### Concept Summary

A package-spotlight experience in which confirmed Social Groups Hospitality Packages receive dedicated, prominent space within an evergreen one-team portfolio. Packages act as concrete planning entry points, while custom and broader planning needs remain equally legitimate.

### Experience Concept

The page establishes the unified team and portfolio, then uses maintained package content to make selected hospitality possibilities tangible. The experience consistently distinguishes defined packages, custom planning, seasonal relevance, and inquiry requirements.

### User Problem Solved

Guests who want a more defined starting point can understand whether a confirmed package may fit, while guests with broader or custom needs can still access the full planning proposition.

### Primary Guest Need

A concrete, current way to begin evaluating social hospitality without mistaking one offer for the full RWLV portfolio.

### Business Role

Support the planned package launch and ongoing package visibility without turning the landing page into a temporary campaign or offer catalog.

### Suggested Page Role

An evergreen social-planning front door with a governed, high-visibility package layer.

### Organizing Model

Package-spotlight within a team-led and guest-need-led portfolio.

### Component Pattern

A conceptual package spotlight connected to eligibility or fit context, supporting proof, related planning capabilities, alternatives for custom needs, qualification, and conditional inquiry.

### Content Strategy

Clearly separate confirmed package facts from general possibilities. State only approved inclusions, availability, audience, timing, terms, and next steps when supplied. Connect packages to relevant needs without inventing names or combinations, and preserve a durable custom-planning path.

### Placement Strategy

Establish the one-team proposition and portfolio first, place the package spotlight early enough to support launch visibility, then continue into broader occasions, capabilities, proof, event windows, qualification, and inquiry.

### Core Sections or Experience Flow

1. **Inspiration:** One-team planning and the breadth of social experiences.
2. **Package spotlight:** Confirmed package information and fit context.
3. **Portfolio context:** Broader occasions and capabilities beyond packages.
4. **Proof:** Relevant property, event, venue, and team evidence.
5. **Current relevance:** Fall, F1, NFR, holiday, wedding, and engagement windows where approved.
6. **Qualification:** Package interest versus custom need, plus group, timing, stay, and hospitality context.
7. **Inquiry:** An honest next step based on confirmed package ownership and general routing.

### Inquiry Strategy
