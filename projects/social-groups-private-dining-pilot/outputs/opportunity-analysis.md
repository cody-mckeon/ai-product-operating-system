# Social Groups / Private Dining — Opportunity Analysis

## Scope And Boundary

This analysis identifies opportunities grounded in the stakeholder request, current-state documentation, live-page evidence, and visual evidence. It treats consolidation as a business and guest-experience opportunity, not as a predetermined design answer.

The opportunities are not scored, ranked, or sequenced. This artifact does not select an information architecture, inquiry destination, page concept, component treatment, or visual direction.

The stakeholder request brings together business areas currently organized by different logics:

| Department | RWLV Meetings & Events; Zouk Private Sales; Social Groups Concierge | Group Dining, Zouk Private Events, concierge-led experiences |
| Occasion | RWLV RFP tabs for Weddings and Celebrations | Weddings, engagements, celebrations, milestones, holiday parties, bachelorette |

| Physical location | RWLV Property Map | Athena, meeting spaces, dining, entertainment, pools |
| Inquiry type | RWLV RFP; Zouk proposal overlay; Concierge request | Meetings, Group Dining, Weddings, Celebrations, venue inquiries |

The opportunity is broader than content consolidation. It includes establishing a coherent relationship among these organizing models while preserving enough distinction for guests to understand what is available, what fits their need, and what happens after inquiry.

## Core Opportunity Tensions

These tensions describe the opportunity space; they do not resolve it.

- **Breadth versus legibility:** the portfolio spans dining, nightlife, lodging, wellness, photography, celebrations, and event-specific hospitality.
- **Occasion versus inventory:** guests may begin with a wedding, milestone, or event weekend, while existing pages largely begin with departments, venues, or services.
- **Unified team versus distributed ownership:** the stakeholder wants one team story, but current evidence shows RWLV destination experts, Social Groups Concierge, Group Dining, Zouk Private Sales, general hotel sales, and separate contact channels.
- **Property credibility versus venue intensity:** RWLV communicates breadth, place, and operational scale; Zouk communicates personality, team expertise, client proof, and nightlife energy.
- **One clear entry versus multiple fulfillment paths:** a unified guest-facing inquiry may still require distinct internal qualification and routing.
- **Evergreen portfolio versus time-bound demand:** the page must account for ongoing occasions alongside August 4 packages, fall demand, F1, and NFR.
- **Inspiration versus qualification:** editorial storytelling and visual atmosphere must coexist with package rules, capacities, room needs, dates, and lead qualification.

## Opportunity 1

### Name

Coherent portfolio identity across fragmented business areas

### Observation

No reviewed page presents Social Groups, Group Dining, Zouk Private Events, Weddings, Celebrations, bachelorette, wellness, Athena engagement shoots, small room blocks, and in-suite hospitality as one connected portfolio. Current content is distributed across RWLV Meetings & Events, Group Dining, Planner Resources, RFP, Our Team, Concierge, the property map, Zouk Private Sales, and FUHU.

### Evidence

- `outputs/current-state.md`, Current Experience Summary and Existing Content Gaps.
- RWLV Meetings & Events includes Group Dining and Social Groups Concierge but remains conference- and venue-led.
- Zouk Private Sales connects multiple Zouk venues under one sales team but does not cover the broader property portfolio.
- Several requested offerings have no supplied source page or screenshot.

### User Impact

Guests with a broad social occasion may need to infer which department, site, or category contains the relevant experience. The relationship among dining, rooms, hospitality, wellness, photography, and nightlife is not visible in one current journey.

### Business Impact

Fragmentation can limit visibility across related revenue areas and obscure the intended message that one planning organization can coordinate experiences across the property.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to establish a clear portfolio identity that makes the relationship among the scoped business areas understandable without erasing their distinct roles, requirements, or fulfillment teams.

### Dependencies

- Confirmed portfolio membership and approved naming.
- Business definition of “Social Groups / Private Dining.”
- Relationship between Group Dining, Zouk Private Sales, Social Groups Concierge, Weddings, hotel sales, and in-suite hospitality.
- Decisions about which offerings are evergreen, seasonal, package-based, or venue-specific.

## Opportunity 2

### Name

Guest-centered orientation across occasion, need, venue, and service

### Observation

Current surfaces use different taxonomies: Meetings & Events uses venue and support categories; Group Dining uses dining setting; Zouk uses venue; RFP uses inquiry type; the property map uses floor. The stakeholder portfolio adds occasion, package, lodging, wellness, and event-window dimensions.

### Evidence

- `outputs/current-state.md`, Existing Portfolio And Category Structure.
- RWLV RFP tabs: Meeting & Events, Group Dining, Weddings, Celebrations.
- Zouk portfolio: The District, Zouk, Ayu, RedTail, FUHU, Here Kitty Kitty.
- Group Dining sections: private rooms, outdoor dining, bars and lounges.
- Known audiences begin with weddings, engagements, celebrations, bachelorette, wellness, room blocks, and F1/NFR needs.

### User Impact

A guest may know the occasion but not the venue, know the desired service but not the department, or know a venue but not the associated planning options. The current landscape does not reveal how those entry points relate.

### Business Impact

An unresolved organizing model can make portfolio breadth appear fragmented or force individual offerings to compete at the same level despite different guest and business roles.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to clarify how guest need, occasion, venue, service, and package dimensions relate so that multiple valid entry intents can lead to a coherent understanding of the portfolio.

### Dependencies

- Audience research or stakeholder evidence about dominant entry intents.
- Search, traffic, inquiry, and conversion data by source page and category.
- Approved taxonomy and definitions for occasions, services, packages, and venues.
- Rules for offerings that belong to more than one category.

## Opportunity 4

There is an opportunity to define the relationship between RWLV property-wide credibility and Zouk's team-, proof-, and portfolio-led storytelling at the level of evidence and brand behavior before visual design begins.

- RWLV and Zouk brand ownership and approval requirements.
- Approved use of Zouk team, client, event, and venue assets on RWLV surfaces.
- Confirmed production components and brand tokens.
- Mobile evidence for both systems.

## Opportunity 5

### Name

Clear inquiry entry across competing actions and destinations

### Observation

Current inquiry and booking actions include RWLV RFP, Zouk proposal overlay, Concierge request, phone, hotel-sales email, chat, Zouk `BOOK`, and the global hotel `BOOK NOW`. Group Dining uses `BOOK YOUR EVENT` and `RESERVE A TABLE` for the same RFP URL. Direct RFP entry defaults to Meeting & Events.

### Evidence

- `outputs/current-state.md`, CTA And Inquiry Path Inventory and Destination Confusion.
- RWLV RFP contains category tabs for Meetings & Events, Group Dining, Weddings, and Celebrations.
- Zouk overlay collects venue, event, group, date, and contact details and states a 48-hour response expectation.
- Social Groups Concierge uses a separate Concierge request path.

### User Impact

Guests may not know whether they are booking a room, reserving a table, requesting an event proposal, contacting concierge, or initiating a Zouk-specific request. The choice can occur before the relationship among those actions is explained.

### Business Impact

Multiple entry points may create duplicate leads, incorrect routing, inconsistent qualification, or incomplete visibility into demand across the combined portfolio.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to clarify the purpose and relationship of inquiry, reservation, concierge, contact, and hotel-booking actions so that guests understand the commitment and destination of each path.

### Dependencies

- Complete inventory of active inquiry channels and owners.
- Destination, field, routing, CRM, SLA, and confirmation-state documentation.
- Evidence about channel performance, abandonment, duplicates, and misrouted leads.
- Rules distinguishing standard dining reservations, private-event inquiries, concierge requests, room booking, and package inquiries.

## Opportunity 6

### Name

Intent-aware qualification and internal routing continuity

### Observation

Existing forms collect different information and reflect different operating assumptions. RWLV RFP supports company events, rooms, meeting space, Group Dining, Weddings, and Celebrations. Zouk's overlay is venue-led. Concierge collects a separate class of requests. No internal routing model or shared lead record was supplied.

### Evidence

- `outputs/current-state.md`, RFP structure and Zouk proposal fields.
- Weddings and Celebrations forms collect sleeping-room needs.
- Group Dining collects nature of event, date/time, venue, and party size.
- Zouk collects venue, company, event type, group size, and timing.
- Post-submit states and CRM handoffs were not observed.

### User Impact

A guest with a combined need—such as rooms, private dining, and wellness—may not know whether one inquiry can represent the complete plan or whether several requests are required.

### Business Impact

Without a verified routing model, a single front-door story may conceal fragmented fulfillment, incomplete lead context, or inconsistent follow-up.

### Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to understand and align guest intent classification, qualification data, internal ownership, and handoff continuity across the portfolio before presenting a unified inquiry promise.

### Dependencies

- Lead-routing and CRM architecture.
- Required data by fulfillment team.
- Rules for multi-service and multi-venue requests.
- Ownership of initial response, qualification, reassignment, and follow-up.
- Response-time commitments and escalation paths.
- Privacy, consent, and data-sharing requirements across RWLV and Zouk.

### Observation

## Opportunity Analysis Takeaway

The consolidation opportunity is the chance to make a fragmented but related business portfolio understandable as one planning capability. The evidence shows that RWLV already supplies property breadth, category structure, environmental credibility, resources, and multi-category inquiry, while Zouk supplies the clearest current team identity, venue-family story, client proof, and repeated proposal posture.

The unresolved work is not merely visual. Portfolio taxonomy, team ownership, package definitions, seasonal rules, content parity, brand governance, and inquiry routing must be made explicit before a design can truthfully express one unified team and one coherent guest journey.

This analysis does not determine what the landing page, navigation, portfolio structure, inquiry model, or visual treatment will be.
