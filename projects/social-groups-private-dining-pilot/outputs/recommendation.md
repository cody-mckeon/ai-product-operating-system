# Recommendation

## Executive Summary

### Top Recommendation

Position the landing page as the front door to **one cross-property planning team for social occasions**, not as a directory of venues or a collection of unrelated offers.

The first organizing model to explore is a **team-led, guest-need/occasion-led hybrid**:

- Lead with the promise that one team can coordinate a social experience across Resorts World Las Vegas.
- Help guests orient around what they are planning and what support they need.
- Use services, packages, and venues as evidence of planning capability rather than as competing top-level stories.
- Keep seasonal and event-window content relevant without allowing it to define the permanent page.
- Present one clear inquiry entry at the guest level while leaving the back-end routing model conditional on operational confirmation.

### Supporting Evidence

- The current experience is fragmented across RWLV Meetings & Events, Group Dining, Social Groups Concierge, RFP, Our Team, Zouk Private Sales, FUHU, phone, email, chat, and hotel booking.
- Zouk Private Sales is the closest existing reference for a unified-team narrative, connected portfolio, client proof, and repeated proposal posture.
- RWLV Meetings & Events is the closest reference for property breadth, resort credibility, category orientation, and cross-property scale.
- No reviewed source currently joins the full portfolio, new Social Groups Hospitality Packages, seasonal demand, a unified team, and a coherent inquiry journey.
- Package details, team ownership, content governance, and lead routing remain unresolved.

### Why It Matters

Guests may begin with a wedding, engagement, celebration, bachelorette trip, group dinner, suite gathering, wellness need, or event weekend—not with knowledge of RWLV's internal departments. A unified planning proposition gives those entry intents one coherent context while preserving the specialist capability behind dining, Zouk, rooms, hospitality, and wellness.

### Priority Order

1. **REC-01 — Establish one planning proposition and a guest-centered portfolio model.**
2. **REC-02 — Define one clear inquiry framework without overstating routing capability.**
3. **REC-03 — Make packages and event windows visible within an evergreen portfolio.**
4. **REC-04 — Combine RWLV property credibility with Zouk's team and proof model.**

## Recommended Page Role

The page should function as the **primary orientation and inquiry entry for planning social occasions across Resorts World Las Vegas**.

It should help a guest answer four questions in sequence at the strategic level:

1. Can this team help plan my kind of occasion?
2. What parts of the property and experience can they coordinate?
3. What evidence gives me confidence that the team can deliver it?
4. What is the correct next step for my planning need?

The page should not attempt to replace every venue detail, package term, resource, booking engine, reservation flow, or operational form. Its role is to create coherent understanding and continuity among them.

## Recommended Organizing Model

Explore a **team-led, guest-need/occasion-led hybrid first**.

### Primary Logic

The stable organizing principle is the guest's planning job: bringing people together for a social occasion with coordinated support across the property.

### Supporting Logic

- **Occasion or need** provides orientation: wedding, engagement, celebration, milestone, holiday gathering, bachelorette, group dinner, or event weekend.
- **Service** explains what can be coordinated: private/group dining, private events, rooms or small blocks, in-suite hospitality, wellness, and photography.
- **Package** provides a defined way to engage when details are confirmed.
- **Venue** supplies choice, atmosphere, capacity, and proof.
- **Season or event window** provides timely relevance without becoming the permanent taxonomy.
- **Team** binds the relationships together and makes the cross-property promise credible.

### Why This Model First

- A venue-led model would reproduce current fragmentation and underrepresent rooms, packages, wellness, and photography.
- A service-led model would align with internal offerings but require guests to understand categories before their occasion.
- A package-led model would overfit the August 4 launch and leave unsupported areas behind.
- A purely occasion-led model could duplicate the same services across many occasions and obscure specialist capability.
- A purely team-led model would establish trust but provide insufficient portfolio orientation.
- The hybrid keeps the team proposition stable while letting guest need organize discovery and letting services, packages, and venues provide evidence.

## Recommended Portfolio Framing

Frame the portfolio as a set of **planning capabilities that can combine around an occasion**, not as an equal-weight list of inventory.

Concept Generation should explore guest-need families that cover, without finalizing labels:

- **Gather and celebrate:** Weddings, engagements, celebrations, milestones, and holiday parties.
- **Dine and host:** Group Dining, private dining, Zouk Private Events, and social gatherings.
- **Stay and entertain:** Small room blocks, suites, in-suite food-and-beverage hospitality, F1, and NFR opportunities.
- **Enhance and commemorate:** Bachelorette, wellness, and Athena engagement photo shoots.
- **Plan through defined packages:** Social Groups Hospitality Packages once their details are approved.

These are strategic coverage groups, not final navigation labels or landing-page copy. Concept Generation may test alternative groupings so long as all portfolio areas remain legible and the same offering is not duplicated without explanation.

Venues should appear as proof and selection context within the relevant planning capability, not as the only way to understand the page.

## Recommended Team Framing

Position the team as a **single planning relationship supported by specialists across the property**.

This framing is more defensible than implying that one person or department directly fulfills every service. It creates room for:

- A shared planning promise.
- Named or role-based specialists where approved.
- Clear acknowledgment of Group Dining, Zouk Private Sales, Social Groups, Weddings, hospitality, and wellness expertise.
- Transparent handoffs that preserve continuity for the guest.
- Proof of cross-team delivery rather than only individual credentials.

The Zouk model supplies the strongest reference for humanizing expertise through people, biographies, client evidence, and event proof. The RWLV model supplies the stronger reference for collective service access and property credibility. The recommended team framing combines those behaviors while remaining conditional on verified ownership.

## Recommended Inquiry Strategy

Recommend **one clear guest-facing planning entry with intent-aware qualification**, not an assumed single back-end form.

### Separate The Journey Into Three Strategic States

1. **Inspiration:** establish the occasion, team promise, property breadth, and relevant possibilities.
2. **Qualification:** provide enough detail about fit, timing, capacity, packages, room needs, inclusions, and constraints for a guest to identify a relevant path.
3. **Inquiry:** present the planning action, required information, responsible response, and next-step expectation.

These are journey responsibilities, not prescribed page sections or interface components.

### Stakeholder Clarification Required

Stakeholders need to select or validate the operational model before Concept Generation treats the inquiry as final:

- One shared form with back-end routing.
- One guest-facing entry that branches into category-specific forms.
- One shared planning team that manually triages requests.
- A clear front door that hands off to existing RWLV, Zouk, or Concierge destinations.

The recommendation does not assume any option is currently feasible.

Standard hotel booking, restaurant reservation, concierge assistance, and general contact can remain available as secondary paths only when their purpose is clearly distinct from social-event planning.

## Recommended Package Strategy

Treat Social Groups Hospitality Packages as a **prominent, maintained expression of the broader planning capability**, not as the page's sole organizing principle.

Before design, each package needs:

- Approved name and audience.
- Guest value and confirmed inclusions.
- Eligibility, dates, availability, and capacity.
- Eligible room or suite context.
- Food-and-beverage scope.
- Pricing posture and terms appropriate for public presentation.
- Imagery and proof.
- Inquiry or booking route.
- Sales owner and response expectation.
- Launch, update, expiration, and archival ownership.

The August 4 date requires immediate clarification: year, publication deadline, commercial launch, availability start, or a combination. Concept Generation should treat all package content as provisional until these facts are supplied.

## Recommended Seasonal / Event-Window Strategy

Keep the page's core proposition evergreen and use fall, F1, NFR, holiday parties, wedding season, and engagement demand as **timely relevance layers**.

Seasonal and event-window content should:

- Connect to a stable planning capability or confirmed package.
- Explain applicable audience, dates, lead times, and constraints.
- Have an owner and expiration rule.
- Preserve relevance when the event window closes.
- Avoid implying inventory or inclusions that are not confirmed.

F1 and NFR should be represented as event-linked in-suite hospitality opportunities only after offer scope, inventory, trademark treatment, and fulfillment are confirmed. Weddings, engagements, milestones, and holiday parties can remain part of the evergreen occasion story while receiving timely emphasis when business priorities warrant it.

- Immersive imagery with clear venue identity.

### Closest Reference

**Use a hybrid.** No single current experience is sufficient:

- **RWLV Meetings & Events:** strongest property breadth; too conference- and venue-led on its own.
- **RWLV Group Dining:** strongest RWLV editorial treatment for one service family; too narrow on its own.
- **Zouk Private Sales:** strongest team, proof, and portfolio narrative; too Zouk-specific and nightlife-forward on its own.
- **FUHU Private Events:** strongest venue-specific operational detail; not a cross-property model.
- **RWLV RFP:** useful inquiry-state reference; not an inspirational landing-page model.

The hybrid must feel like one RWLV experience, not adjacent RWLV and Zouk sections stitched together.

## Risks To Carry Forward

1. **False unity:** the page promises one team while operations remain fragmented or invisible.
2. **Taxonomy overload:** occasion, service, venue, package, and season all compete as equal organizing systems.
3. **Inquiry mismatch:** a single CTA sends guests to an irrelevant default form or loses their source intent.
4. **Package readiness:** August 4 visibility is designed before package facts, terms, assets, or routing are approved.
5. **Seasonal staleness:** F1, NFR, fall, or holiday content remains visible beyond its valid window.
6. **Property dilution:** a Zouk-inspired direction weakens RWLV recognition or implies the portfolio is nightlife-led.
7. **Zouk dilution:** Zouk venues lose their distinct atmosphere and specialist credibility inside a generic property treatment.
8. **Uneven evidence:** well-documented venues dominate while room blocks, hospitality, wellness, Weddings, bachelorette, or photography remain vague.
9. **Proof governance:** testimonials, client marks, portraits, or past-event imagery are used outside approved scope.
10. **Equal-weight inventory:** a long grid makes every offering appear interchangeable and obscures business and guest priority.
11. **Mobile density:** the breadth of the portfolio becomes difficult to scan or inquire about on smaller screens.
12. **Duplicate ownership:** multiple teams respond to, re-enter, or lose the same multi-service lead.
13. **Search and content duplication:** consolidated summaries and existing detail pages conflict or become stale at different rates.
14. **Unsupported bundling:** coordinated services are presented as packages when commercial or operational bundling is not confirmed.

## Not Recommended

- A direct clone of Zouk Private Sales.
- A reskin or expansion of RWLV Meetings & Events that keeps the experience conference-first.
- A venue directory as the primary page purpose.
- A package-first page built around the August 4 launch.
- A temporary F1, NFR, fall, or holiday campaign page as the permanent destination.
- A flat, equal-weight grid of every offering.
- A page organized primarily around internal departments.
- A nightlife-heavy aesthetic applied to Weddings, wellness, engagements, or room-block content.
- A mood-led page that lacks capacities, eligibility, timing, and operational detail.
- A universal proposal form or single back-end route assumed without stakeholder confirmation.
- Multiple competing primary inquiry actions with unexplained destinations.
- Final copy, proof, or imagery based on package and offering details that have not been approved.
