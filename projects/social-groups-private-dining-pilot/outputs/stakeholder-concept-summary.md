# Social Groups / Private Dining Landing Page Concept Summary

## Overview

The v4 concept explores a dedicated Resorts World Las Vegas landing page for
Social Groups / Private Dining. Its purpose is to give a broad, currently
fragmented portfolio one clear place to be understood—without turning the page
into a venue directory, package campaign, or long inquiry form.

The concept is not a final design. It establishes the proposed page role,
story, hierarchy, and tone so stakeholders can align on the direction before
final content, proof, package details, ownership, and inquiry routing are
defined.

V4 made meaningful progress toward a stakeholder-readable concept. The
strategic direction remains sound and is ready for directional stakeholder
alignment. Bounded refinement can follow once the key business, content, and
operating decisions are made.

## Why We Explored This Direction

Today, related social-planning content is distributed across RWLV Meetings &
Events, Group Dining, Social Groups Concierge, the Request for Proposal
experience, Zouk Private Sales, individual venue pages, and separate booking or
support paths. Each surface explains part of the story, but no single experience
shows how the broader portfolio fits together.

Guests are also more likely to begin with an occasion or practical need—a
celebration, private dinner, wedding, group stay, or hospitality need—than with
knowledge of the department or venue responsible for it.

The selected direction responds to that gap by combining:

- A coherent planning proposition.
- Guest-centered occasion and need discovery.
- RWLV property breadth and editorial warmth.
- Human expertise and proof behaviors inspired by Zouk Private Sales, without
  copying its nightlife-led visual identity.
- A governed place for future packages and timely opportunities.
- A clearer transition toward inquiry while operational routing remains open.

## Core Concept

The core concept is **One Team, Many Occasions**: one coherent planning
relationship that helps guests understand the possibilities across the
property.

This is a proposed guest experience, not confirmation that one team, one owner,
one form, or one operating system currently handles every request. The final
experience may involve multiple specialists or handoffs. Stakeholders still
need to define the operating model and the language that can truthfully describe
it.

The planning relationship is the stable story. Occasions and practical needs
organize discovery. Capabilities, property settings, approved proof, packages,
and seasonal relevance support that story rather than competing with it.

## What The Page Is Designed To Do

The page is intended to help a prospective guest:

- Understand the Social Groups / Private Dining proposition quickly.
- Begin with what they are planning rather than an internal department.
- See the breadth of relevant possibilities without navigating a dense catalog.
- Gain confidence through approved team, property, event, and operational
  evidence.
- Discover approved packages or timely opportunities when they are available.
- Understand what information may help prepare a planning conversation.
- Reach a clear next step while keeping hotel reservations, table reservations,
  property support, and event planning appropriately distinct.

The page is an orientation and inquiry entry. It is not intended to replace
venue detail pages, package terms, hotel booking, restaurant reservations,
resource libraries, or operational forms.

## Proposed Experience Flow

The concept uses a seven-stage journey:

1. **Arrival and orientation** — Introduce the social-planning purpose and the
   idea of bringing different occasion needs together at RWLV.
2. **Occasion or need recognition** — Help guests begin with what they are
   planning through a curated editorial experience rather than a grid of equal
   options.
3. **Property breadth** — Show how dining, stays, gathering spaces, and resort
   experiences may relate, without presenting an approved service bundle.
4. **Confidence and proof** — Demonstrate how approved planning roles, property
   context, event evidence, and operational detail could build trust.
5. **Packages and current relevance** — Reserve a visible but supporting place
   for approved packages or timely opportunities while keeping the evergreen
   page useful when no package is active.
6. **Planning preparation** — Help guests understand the kinds of context that
   may shape a planning conversation without presenting a final field set or
   form.
7. **Inquiry continuity** — Offer a clear planning next step while preserving
   distinct purposes for event planning, hotel reservations, table
   reservations, and property support.

## What This Solves

The direction addresses several connected problems:

- **Fragmented storytelling:** It gives the portfolio one coherent front door.
- **Department-first complexity:** It lets guests begin with their occasion or
  need instead of navigating internal structures.
- **Uneven portfolio visibility:** It creates a way to show breadth without
  giving every venue, service, or season equal weight.
- **Limited trust context:** It establishes a place for approved people,
  property, event, and operational proof near the claims they support.
- **Package readiness:** It reserves a governed, maintainable role for future
  package content without making an unapproved offer look live.
- **Inquiry confusion:** It creates a clearer guest-facing planning intent while
  leaving the final destination and routing model to be confirmed.
- **Brand imbalance:** It combines RWLV hospitality and property context with
  the strongest team-and-proof behaviors observed in Zouk, without becoming a
  Zouk clone or nightlife-dominant page.

## What Is Directional

V4 uses directional headlines, supporting copy, category labels, images, and
action language to make the concept understandable. These elements demonstrate
the intended tone and hierarchy; they are not approved production content.

The following remain directional:

- Page and section copy.
- Occasion and practical-need groupings.
- Capability categories and examples.
- Planning-preparation topics.
- Image selections and crops.
- Team and proof structures.
- CTA labels and placements.
- Package and custom-planning presentation.
- Visual styling beyond confirmed RWLV guidance.
- Desktop composition and all responsive behavior.

Annotations in the concept are intended to keep these open items visible
without overwhelming the main experience. Their placement and legibility still
need refinement.

## What Is Not Final

The concept does not approve or establish:

- A single team, department, owner, form, route, CRM process, response time, or
  handoff model.
- Final occasion taxonomy, capability inventory, service availability, or
  combinations of experiences.
- Any package name, audience, price, value, inclusion, exclusion, eligibility,
  term, date, availability, inventory, proof, owner, or inquiry path.
- The year or operational meaning of the August 4 package timing.
- Final seasonal or event-window content for fall, F1, NFR, holidays, weddings,
  or engagements.
- Team members, titles, biographies, testimonials, client names, event outcomes,
  awards, or operational proof.
- Final inquiry labels, destinations, form fields, routing, confirmation, or
  follow-up behavior.
- Final imagery, asset rights, production components, responsive layouts, or
  accessibility compliance.

## Stakeholder Decisions Needed

Stakeholder alignment is needed on:

1. **Planning relationship:** What can the business truthfully promise, who owns
   the first guest relationship, and where do specialist handoffs occur?
2. **Portfolio scope:** Which capabilities and offerings belong on the page,
   which are highest priority, and what constraints must be visible?
3. **Occasion and need model:** Which audience entry points should lead the
   experience, and what language is approved?
4. **Packages:** What is launching, for whom, with what value, inclusions,
   terms, availability, timing, ownership, and next step?
5. **Inquiry model:** Should the guest encounter shared intake, category-based
   forms, manual triage, or transparent handoffs to existing destinations?
6. **Proof strategy:** Which team, event, property, client, and operational
   evidence is approved and representative of the full portfolio?
7. **RWLV/Zouk relationship:** Which Zouk storytelling behaviors and assets may
   be used within an RWLV-governed experience?
8. **Content governance:** Who approves, maintains, updates, and retires package,
   seasonal, team, proof, and operational content?

## Content And Asset Needs

The next design phase will need:

- Approved page and section messaging.
- Confirmed occasion/need taxonomy and capability descriptions.
- Final package content, commercial terms, status, dates, and ownership.
- A documented inquiry and handoff model, including distinct booking,
  reservation, planning, and support destinations.
- Approved RWLV property, dining, social-occasion, hospitality, and lifestyle
  imagery with source, rights, crop, and replacement guidance.
- Approved team or role framing, portraits, and biographies if people will be
  featured.
- Approved event, client, property, and operational proof with rights,
  relevance, freshness, and owners.
- Authoritative RWLV global header/footer references and production design
  system assets.
- Desktop and mobile content priorities, including annotation behavior for the
  review concept.
- Legal, brand, content, operations, and accessibility review requirements.

## Recommended Next Step

Use this concept to align with stakeholders on the overall direction before
moving into final design, copy, and implementation planning.

The stakeholder discussion should focus on:

- Whether the “one planning relationship” direction accurately reflects the
  business goal.
- Which occasions, needs, and offerings should be prioritized on the page.
- How Social Groups Hospitality Packages should be represented once details
  are approved.
- What inquiry path, routing model, and ownership structure should be used.
- Which team, proof, event, and property assets can support the page credibly.

After stakeholder alignment, the next design revision should preserve the
seven-stage flow while:

- Completing the desktop and mobile concept.
- Making the final inquiry section more readable.
- Refining directional copy and annotation placement.
- Strengthening RWLV visual cues and global page context.
- Replacing placeholder imagery with approved assets where available.
- Clarifying package, proof, and inquiry dependencies.

The work does not need a new strategic concept. It needs stakeholder
validation, approved content inputs, and bounded visual refinement.
